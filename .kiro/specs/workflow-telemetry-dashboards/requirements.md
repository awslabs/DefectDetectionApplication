# Requirements Document

## Introduction

DDA workflows run on Greengrass core devices and publish results to AWS IoT Core through the `mqtt_publish` output node. Nothing in the product reads those messages back: no Portal IoT rule matches a workflow topic (the Portal's rules forward named-shadow documents only), the Portal's landing page (`frontend/src/pages/Dashboard.tsx`) shows counts and a hard-coded "Recent Events" list, and the device-local `hmi/` app polls the LocalServer run API.

The owner asked for dashboards populated from the workflows' IoT data, the ability to watch the live feed with bounding boxes, and clips of violations for the PPE demo. After a Quick Sight design was costed, the owner redirected (2026-10-15):

> "redirect to portal charts and summaries. I would however, like a debug mode that sends all data viewable as a log somewhere when the workflow is misbehaving. It doesn't need to be part of the dashboards."

This specification is the first half of that redirect. **Binding decisions** (recorded by the orchestrator from the owner's answers; they are not open for review):

- **Portal-native dashboards** on the Portal's existing Cloudscape charts (`@cloudscape-design/components` ^3.0.0, installed 3.0.1143, which ships line, bar, area, pie and mixed line-bar charts plus a `DateRangePicker`).
- Fed by **periodic summaries published by the device** (default every 10 s per (device, workflow), configurable) plus **discrete events published immediately** (violation confirmed, clip ready, run error), not by per-frame messages.
- **No Amazon Quick Sight in this phase**: no edition change, no Quick Sight users, datasets, dashboards or embedding. A low-cost S3 data lake is kept through Amazon Data Firehose so Quick Sight or Athena can be added later without re-plumbing; that path is documented only.
- The **debug mode is a separate capability** with its own specification (`workflow-debug-mode`). It never feeds and never appears on the dashboards.

**The first consumer** is the PPE video-loop demo workflow on the MIC-730 (JetPack 5). Today its data leaves the device three ways: the workflow's `mqtt_publish` node sends `{"detection_count": "N"}` on `ppe-demo/mic730/result` with no `schema` field; a device-side clip helper (demo tooling outside this repository) sends per-frame `ppe-demo.status.v1` messages (about 520 B, about 1.7 per second) on `ppe-demo/mic730/status` and one `ppe-demo.clip.v1` event per violation clip on `ppe-demo/mic730/clip`, with a follow-up event carrying a higher `revision` when a retried S3 upload succeeds. The workflow's trigger topic is `ppe-demo/mic730/invoke`, so the filter `ppe-demo/mic730/+` would also match the trigger. The helper publishes and uploads with the device's broad token-exchange-role credentials, which the `portal-authorization-and-iam-scoping` specification (task 6.5) detaches; product publishing must therefore go through Greengrass IPC with component access control, never that policy.

**Facts that shape the requirements** (read in the worktree on 2026-10-15 and re-read on 2026-10-16):

- `mqtt_publish` publishes a literal topic (`output_bindings.py`, `str(parameters["topic"])`) through the process-wide shared Greengrass IPC client (`utils/ipc_client.py`) with `PublishToIoTCore`. **All seven** LocalServer recipes (`recipe.yaml`, `recipe-amd64.yaml`, `recipe-amd64-nvidia.yaml`, `recipe-arm64.yaml`, `recipe-arm64-jp5.yaml`, `recipe-arm64-jp6.yaml`, `recipe-arm64-jp7.yaml`) authorize `aws.greengrass#PublishToIoTCore` on `*` (`mqttproxy:2`) and both `PublishToIoTCore` and `SubscribeToIoTCore` on `$aws/things/*/shadow/name/*` (`mqttproxy:1`). *(At the time of writing `recipe-amd64-nvidia.yaml` carried `mqttproxy:1` only, so on that variant — a live Build Fleet target, `build_domain.py` ~74–75 — every `PublishToIoTCore` outside the shadow topics, including the `mqtt_publish` nodes, was denied. Resolved: the `amd64Nvidia` recipe carries `mqttproxy:2` since the trunk integration, device verification pending with the first `amd64Nvidia` build; A10/Q8.)* `SubscribeToIoTCore` is deliberately not broadened beyond the shadow topics.
- The Greengrass nucleus connects to AWS IoT Core with the core device's thing name as the MQTT client id, with a `#<n>` suffix after every 50 subscriptions.
- The executor (`workflow_engine/pipeline_executor.py`) already writes per-run artifacts under `/aws_dda/captures/{workflowId}/{executionId}/` and persists `node_status_json`, `log_path` and `trigger_context_json` on the `WorkflowExecution` row, whose `started_at` and `finished_at` are integer **seconds**. `started_at` is set at enqueue (`api.py` ~288, `trigger_runtime.py` ~543, `continuous_runner.py` ~167) and then **overwritten with the RUNNING time** at `pipeline_executor.py` ~1947–1948, after the pending check (~1547), preflight, document load, frame-source staging and the per-run readiness gate (`_await_model_readiness`, ~1843, which waits up to `dda_triton.model_readiness.READY_TIMEOUT_S = 600` s for a loading model); the enqueue time must therefore be captured before that overwrite, and a run's "pipeline time" begins only at the RUNNING transition. The trigger gate (`trigger_runtime.TriggerGate.fire`) discards firings without counting them; the continuous runner keeps per-registration counters (`started`, `completed`, `failed`, `skippedBusy`, `skippedNoNewFrame`, `modelUnavailable`, the last incremented once per wait by `_note_model_wait` when the previous wait was None) and records its model wait as a `ModelWait` document; the per-run readiness gate returns only the failure message and discards the `ReadinessOutcome`'s `state` and `waited_seconds`; the model gate (`model_gate.py`) has seven states: Triton's `READY`, `LOADING`, `UNLOADING`, `UNAVAILABLE`, `UNKNOWN` and the gate's own `NOT_DEPLOYED`, `INCOMPLETE`.
- Two LocalServer agents already apply Portal-originated named-shadow state without a deployment: `camera_sync/agent.py` and `user_accounts_sync/agent.py`, each with a `SubscriptionHandler` on `$aws/things/{thing}/shadow/name/{shadow}/update/` and a catch-up when the subscription becomes active. **Their catch-up reads the current document through ShadowManager IPC** (`IoTShadowAccessor.get_thing_shadow_state_request`, `camera_sync/agent.py` ~1065), not over MQTT; the `SubscriptionHandler` does publish an empty `.../get` on activation (~310), but no live code consumes the `get/accepted` answer (only the unused `CloudIoTShadowAccessor` did). The MQTT `get` → `get/accepted` read this feature relies on is therefore **new code**, not a proven pattern, and RB-7 verifies the round trip on the device. The ShadowManager `synchronize` list that the Portal's deployments set (`deployments.py`, `portal_shadow_sync_config`) names `dda-camera-registry`, `dda-camera-bindings` and `dda-model-status` only; extending it needs a deployment, which is why the MQTT path is used.
- The packaged `manifest.json` (`workflow_packaging.py`) carries `workflowId`, `workflowName`, `workflowVersion` and `targetArch` but **no use-case id**.
- The Portal lists a use case's devices from Greengrass (`ListCoreDevices` plus the `dda-portal:managed=true` tag in the use case's account), not from a table. The Devices table (`dda-portal-devices`, keyed by `device_id`, the thing name) holds a row with `usecase_id` only for devices that completed quick setup (`quick_setup.py`) or were given attributes through `PUT /devices/{id}` (`devices.update_device_flags`, ~123–215: it needs `Permission.NODE_DESIGNER_MANAGE`, held by UseCaseAdmin and PortalAdmin only; it requires `test_device` or `target_architecture` in the body, answering 400 otherwise; it checks the caller's permission on the `usecase_id` **in the body** and sets the row's `usecase_id` to it unconditionally; the UI calls it from the device page's "Target architecture" control, `DeviceDetail.tsx` ~297–325); a device enrolled another way has no row. Its GSI `usecase-devices-index` has the sort key `last_heartbeat`, which nothing writes, so the index is sparse and empty and nothing queries it. The Workflows table carries `usecase_id`; the Deployments table lets `workflow_tuning.registered_devices(usecase_id, workflow_id)` name the devices the Portal records as running a workflow.
- The Portal's RBAC is `shared_utils.Permission` with the role matrix in `RBACManager`, `rbac_manager.has_permission(user_id, usecase_id, permission, user_info)`, and the registry-enforcement switch `PORTAL_REGISTRY_ENFORCED` (default off; with it off, every signed-in user resolves to at least Viewer on every use case).
- AWS IoT rules can invoke a Lambda function in another account through the function's resource-based policy, asynchronously, when the function is in the rule's region; the SQS, SNS, S3 and Lambda actions are the cross-account-capable destinations. A Greengrass deployment to a device restarts LocalServer and every model component and can wedge Triton until the `triton-model-cleanup-wedge` fix ships.

**Three classes of work.**
- **Portal changes**, implemented and verified without deploying: a new CDK stack, Lambda handlers, the shared layer, the frontend, docs and tests.
- **Edge (LocalServer) changes**, implemented and unit-tested, but not built, deployed or committed until a later owner-gated phase verifies them on a device (`.kiro/steering/builds.md`).
- **Account operations**, as Runbook_Tasks of the owner-gated phase: the Portal deploy, the use-case account template update, the media lifecycle rules, enabling telemetry for the demo use case and the live verification.

### Decisions and assumptions

Each is a default the design may refine with reasons and the owner gate confirms.

- **A1. Scale.** Costs and capacities use Demo_Scale and Fleet_Scale (Glossary).
- **A2. Intervals.** Summary interval 10 s (5–60 s). Idle heartbeat every 60 s. Tile polling: Now and Health 5 s, clip gallery 15 s, live tile 2 s, charts 60 s at minute resolution, 300 s at hour resolution and 900 s at day resolution (one series request per Dashboard per refresh, Requirement 9.4).
- **A3. Retention.** Summaries 48 h; minute aggregates 7 days; hour and day aggregates 400 days; events 30 days; clip entries and clips 90 days (7–365); live frames under 24 h; lake 400 days.
- **A4. Freshness.** Now panel and Health panel: within one Interval plus one poll plus 2 s of transit (about 17 s) at p95, and about 10 s typically; live tile: 5 s at p95; clip gallery: 30 s; minute charts: within 2 minutes of the minute closing.
- **A5. Roles.** View dashboards: Viewer, Operator, DataScientist, UseCaseAdmin, PortalAdmin. Manage dashboards: Operator, DataScientist, UseCaseAdmin, PortalAdmin. Manage telemetry settings: UseCaseAdmin, PortalAdmin. DataLabeler: none.
- **A6. Dashboard scope.** One use case, one workflow, up to 20 of the use case's devices. No cross-use-case views.
- **A7. Attribution.** The device comes from the publishing certificate and the nucleus client id; the use case from the device's Device_Record; the topic is a cross-check. A device without a Device_Record for the use case publishes nothing useful (its Control_Shadow is never written) and anything it does publish is rejected; the readiness checklist names such devices and the Device_Record_Remedy.
- **A8. Media default.** Clips, live frames and debug images go to the use case's Results_Bucket, the bucket the device role can still write to after the device role is narrowed.
- **A9. Latency semantics.** A run's `latency` is **pipeline time**: from the executor's RUNNING transition (after preflight, frame-source staging and the Triton readiness gate) to the end of the run. The readiness wait is carried separately (`model.waits`, `model.waitedMs`), and the queue wait (`queued_ms`) and the pre-pipeline setup time (`setup_ms`) are carried on the run hook for the debug mode only, so that a cold-model run (up to 600 s in the readiness gate) does not dominate the latency chart while the same episode is visible in the model panel.
- **A10. Architecture support.** **Resolved: the `amd64Nvidia` recipe carries `mqttproxy:2` since the trunk integration (device verification pending with the first `amd64Nvidia` build).** Telemetry (and the debug mode) are supported on all seven LocalServer variants, whose recipes carry `mqttproxy:2` (pinned by the recipe test, whose exception list is empty). *Originally: supported on the six variants whose recipe carried the statement; the `amd64Nvidia` variant unsupported until its recipe gained it, a separate owner-gated recipe change (Q8), not part of this feature.* **Vocabulary:** `amd64Nvidia` is the LocalServer component suffix and Build Fleet target (`build_domain.py` ~74–75, recipe `recipe-amd64-nvidia.yaml`); the Devices table records the same hardware as `target_architecture = "x86_64_nvidia"` (`devices.py` `TARGET_ARCHITECTURES`: `x86_64`, `x86_64_nvidia`, `arm64_cpu`, `arm64_jp5`, `arm64_jp6`, `arm64_jp7`; `update_device_flags` rejects any other value). Every Portal-side check for this variant compares against `x86_64_nvidia`; the device does not detect its own variant.

### Out of scope

- Amazon Quick Sight assets, users, edition changes or embedding (documented as a future add-on only, Requirement 16).
- Changing the PPE demo's producers inside this repository. The clip helper's adoption of the product contracts is demo tooling tracked outside the repository (Requirement 13).
- Ingesting legacy topics through Topic_Bindings. Exact-filter bindings stay a documented option from the prior design; this phase ingests the convention topics only.
- The debug mode (its own specification).
- Alerts and notifications (e-mail, SNS, SMS).
- Fleet-wide views across use cases, including PortalAdmin aggregate views.
- Custom authoring by Portal users; new templates are code changes.
- Redaction of people in clips or frames.
- Backfill of telemetry published before a use case enables dashboards.
- The landing page and the existing `/captures` routes and device Results tab.
- Any deployment or AWS resource creation (the owner-gated phase).

## Glossary

Terms defined in other specifications keep their definitions there: from `portal-usecase-bucket-config`, Use_Case, Allowed_Bucket_Set, Portal_Owned_Buckets and Refusal; from `portal-authorization-and-iam-scoping`, Route_Auth_Inventory, Runbook_Task and Device_Role.

**Platform and records**
- **Portal**: the Edge CV Portal (`edge-cv-portal/`): its CDK stacks, the Lambda handlers behind API Gateway with the Cognito authorizer, the shared layer and the React frontend.
- **Device**: a Greengrass core device the Portal lists for a use case (`GET /devices?usecase_id=`), tagged `dda-portal:managed=true` in the use case's account and region, identified by its thing name.
- **Device_Record**: the Devices table row `device_id = <thing name>` whose `usecase_id` names the use case. It is the only record that binds a thing to exactly one use case (in a shared account two use cases list the same Devices), so Attribution and every Control_Shadow write need it. `usecase_devices(usecase)` is the committed lookup: a filtered `Scan` of the Devices table, never the empty `usecase-devices-index`. A Device without a Device_Record is a "device record missing" readiness item with the Device_Record_Remedy.
- **Device_Record_Remedy**: the one way to create or correct a Device_Record with the routes that exist today: "A UseCaseAdmin (or PortalAdmin) sets the device's **Target architecture** on its device page (Devices → <device>), which calls `PUT /devices/{id}` with `{"usecase_id": "<uc>", "target_architecture": "<arch>"}`; the equivalent call with `{"usecase_id": "<uc>", "test_device": false}` also works; re-running quick setup on the device records it as well. The route needs `node_designer:manage`, which Operators do not hold." A body with `usecase_id` alone is refused by that route (400 `test_device or target_architecture required`).
- **Workflow**: a Portal workflow record (Workflows table, `workflow_id`, carrying `usecase_id`) and its versions.
- **Pair**: one (Device, Workflow) combination; the unit a summary describes.
- **Telemetry_Settings**: a use case's dashboard settings: whether telemetry is enabled, the summary interval, the idle heartbeat interval, the Media_Location, and when and by whom they last changed (Requirement 6).
- **Control_Shadow**: the named shadow `dda-workflow-telemetry` of a Device, whose `desired` state carries the use case id, the enablement, the intervals and the Live_Leases, written by the Portal and read by the Telemetry_Agent (Requirement 5).

**Producers and messages**
- **Telemetry_Agent**: the LocalServer service this specification adds to the workflow engine: it observes every run, builds Summaries and Events, holds live frames under a lease, applies the Control_Shadow and publishes through Greengrass IPC (Requirement 5).
- **Producer**: anything that publishes Telemetry_Messages for a Pair: the Telemetry_Agent (producer name `engine`) or a Helper.
- **Helper**: a device-side process beside the workflow that publishes an extension, such as the PPE demo's clip helper (producer name `ppe-clipper`).
- **Telemetry_Message**: an AWS IoT Core message on a Convention_Topic that this feature ingests: a Summary, an Event or a Live_Frame message.
- **Summary**: one `dda.summary.v1` message describing a Pair over one Interval (Requirement 2).
- **Interval**: a wall-clock-aligned window of `summaryIntervalS` seconds.
- **Heartbeat**: a Summary with `idle: true` and no run data, sent while a Pair has had no run for 5 minutes or has never run since its registration appeared (Requirement 2.9).
- **Event**: one `dda.event.v1` message, published immediately: `violation_confirmed`, `clip_ready` or `run_error` (Requirement 3).
- **Live_Frame**: a `dda.live.v1` message plus the annotated JPEG it points to, sent while a Live_Lease is active (Requirement 10).
- **Live_Lease**: a time-bounded grant written into the Control_Shadow that lets the Telemetry_Agent send Live_Frames for one Pair while someone is watching.
- **Convention_Topic**: `dda/telemetry/{usecase_id}/{workflow_id}/{kind}`, kind ∈ {`summary`, `event`, `live`} (Requirement 4).
- **Schema_Id**: the `schema` value of a message (`dda.summary.v1`, `dda.event.v1`, `dda.live.v1`).
- **Schema_Registry**: the committed definition of each Schema_Id: required and optional fields, types, limits and the measures it feeds.
- **Extension**: a named block under `ext` in a Summary or Event that a Helper owns, for example `ext.ppe` (Requirement 2.6).
- **Publisher_Identity**: what AWS IoT Core reports about the publishing connection in rule SQL: `principal()` (the certificate thumbprint) and `clientid()`.
- **Attribution**: the Device and use case a message is assigned to, with its basis (Requirement 7).

**Stores**
- **Summary_Store**: the DynamoDB table holding accepted Summaries and the live-frame pointers, keyed by Pair and receive time (Requirement 8).
- **Aggregate_Store**: the DynamoDB table holding minute, hour and day aggregates per Pair (Requirement 8).
- **Event_Store**: the DynamoDB table holding Events and the Clip_Index (Requirement 8).
- **Clip_Index**: the Event_Store's per-clip entries, one per `clipId`, carrying the newest `revision` (Requirement 3.4).
- **Lake**: the Portal-owned S3 bucket that Data Firehose fills with accepted Summaries and Events, for the future add-on (Requirement 8.6, Requirement 16).

**Buckets and media**
- **Results_Bucket**: the use case's inference-results bucket: its `inference_uploader_s3_bucket` override when set, otherwise the account's `dda-inference-results-<account>` bucket (the resolution `captures.py` makes).
- **Media_Location**: the Results_Bucket and the key layout under `dda-telemetry/` that hold a use case's clips and live frames (Requirement 14).

**Dashboards and tiles**
- **Dashboard**: a Portal record naming a use case, a Workflow, one to 20 of the use case's Devices, a Dashboard_Template and a name.
- **Dashboard_Template**: a committed template: the measures it charts, the tiles it shows and the kinds and Extensions it needs (Requirement 9).
- **Measure_Catalog**: the committed list of measures (field path, aggregation, unit, label) the templates draw from (Requirement 9.2).
- **Tiles**: the Now_Panel, the Health_Panel, the Clip_Gallery, the Live_Tile and the chart panels of a Dashboard.
- **Now_Panel**: the latest values of each Device of the Dashboard (Requirement 11).
- **Health_Panel**: each Device's flow state, gap and drop counters, model state and core-device status (Requirement 11).
- **Clip_Gallery**: the list and player of the Dashboard's clips (Requirement 12).
- **Live_Tile**: the latest annotated frame of one Device (Requirement 10).

**Topology and sizing**
- **Topology**: `portal` when the use case's `account_id` is the Portal account, else `cross` (Requirement 7.6).
- **Freshness**: the time from AWS IoT Core receiving a message to its first display in the Portal; for a Live_Frame, from capture.
- **Demo_Scale**: 1 Device and 1 Workflow running 8 h a day; about 100 Events an hour; 1–3 viewers and 1 Live_Tile; 130 tile viewer-hours and 44 live viewer-hours a month.
- **Fleet_Scale**: 100 Devices and 300 Pairs over a 10-hour working day; about 2,100 Events an hour; 50 viewers; 2,200 tile viewer-hours and 2,200 live viewer-hours (10 concurrent Live_Tiles) a month.
- **$D**: the delivery workflow's uncommitted state directory, holding the live identifiers the runbooks need; never committed.

## Requirements

### Requirement 1: Portal-native dashboards on Cloudscape

**User Story:** As the owner, I want workflow dashboards built into the Portal with its existing chart components, so that I get quick dashboards without a BI subscription, a console step or a deployment.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. THE Portal SHALL render every Dashboard natively with `@cloudscape-design/components` (line, bar, area, pie and mixed line-bar charts, `DateRangePicker`, `KeyValuePairs`, status indicators), plus an HTML `<img>` for the Live_Tile and `<video>` for clip playback. THE design SHALL add a charting dependency only where it shows Cloudscape provably cannot do the job, and SHALL pin that dependency's exact version.
2. WHEN a holder of `dashboards:manage` creates a Dashboard, THE Portal SHALL need exactly these inputs: a use case, one to 20 of its Devices, a Workflow of the use case, a Dashboard_Template and a name (1–100 characters, unique per use case after NFKC case folding). No AWS console step and no deployment SHALL be needed. A `device_id` without a Device_Record naming the use case SHALL be refused with 400 `device_not_recorded` and the Device_Record_Remedy as its `remedy`.
3. THE Dashboard_Templates SHALL live in committed code over the Measure_Catalog (Requirement 9). New templates and measures SHALL be code changes.
4. THE Dashboard page SHALL show, from one Dashboard record: the chart panels of its template over a selectable time range (relative presets 15 minutes, 1 hour, 6 hours, 24 hours, 7 days, 30 days, and an absolute range up to 400 days, served at three resolutions so that no request exceeds the 1,440-points-per-Device cap of Requirement 9.4: minute items for ranges up to 6 hours, hour items up to 60 days, day items beyond, Requirement 8.2), the Now_Panel, the Health_Panel, the Clip_Gallery when the template includes it, and the Live_Tile.
5. THE design SHALL briefly record why Quick Sight is not used in this phase (the owner's decision and the cost comparison of Requirement 15) and SHALL carry the alternatives considered by the prior design as a one-paragraph note each: Quick Sight, IoT SiteWise Monitor (not available to new customers), Amazon Managed Grafana, CloudWatch dashboards and the device-local `hmi/` app.

### Requirement 2: The summary contract, `dda.summary.v1`

**User Story:** As a workflow author, I want one versioned summary that any workflow can publish without per-workflow setup, so that dashboards show runs, latency, detections and model state for every workflow, with PPE-specific fields as an extension.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. THE Summary SHALL be a JSON object of at most 16 KB with `schema: "dda.summary.v1"`, and SHALL carry: `intervalStart` and `intervalEnd` (epoch milliseconds; the Producer SHALL align `intervalEnd` to a wall-clock multiple of `intervalS`, **an obligation of the Producer that the ingestion does not validate**, so that a Helper whose clock drifts is charted at its receive time rather than rejected; the ingestion validates only the length rule `1000 ≤ intervalEnd − intervalStart ≤ intervalS × 1000 + 1000`, so that a producer starting mid-interval may emit a short first interval and is never rejected for it; a producer that starts less than 1 s before a boundary SHALL skip that partial window and fold it into the next, so that no Summary is ever emitted below the 1 s floor); `emittedAt`; `deviceId` (the thing name), `workflowId`, `workflowVersion` (string); `producer: {name, version, instance}` where `instance` is a UUID chosen at producer start; `seq`, an integer that starts at 1 per `workflowId` within an `instance` and increases by one per Summary **or Heartbeat** of that workflow; `intervalS` (the window length this message declares: `summaryIntervalS` for a Summary, `idleHeartbeatS` for a Heartbeat; **validated by kind**: 5–60 when `idle` is false, 30–300 when `idle` is true, so that a Summary declaring `intervalS: 120` is `schema_invalid`); and `idle` (boolean).
2. THE Summary from the `engine` producer SHALL carry `runs: {completed, failed, dropped}` for runs that finished in the Interval, where `dropped` counts trigger firings discarded by the trigger gate (queue full or `drop` policy) plus continuous ticks skipped because a run was in progress (`skippedBusy`); and `latency: {p50Ms, p95Ms, maxMs, n}` over the Interval's finished runs that reached the pipeline, where each run's latency is **pipeline time**: `finished_ms − run_started_ms` in milliseconds, both stamped by the executor itself (`run_started_ms` at the RUNNING transition, `pipeline_executor.py` ~1947, **after** preflight, document load, frame-source staging and the Triton readiness gate; `finished_ms` in the `finally` block), so that queue wait, setup and model-readiness waits are excluded and the second-resolution `started_at`/`finished_at` columns are not used. A run refused before the RUNNING transition (a preflight or validation failure, or a readiness-gate failure) SHALL count in `runs.failed` and SHALL contribute no latency sample (`run_started_ms` is `None`; `latency.n` counts only runs with a sample). THE run hook SHALL carry, for the debug mode and never for the latency measures: `enqueued_at_s` (the row's `started_at` captured right after the pending check, **before** ~1948 overwrites it), `accepted_ms` (stamped right after the pending check), `queued_ms` = `max(0, accepted_ms − enqueued_at_s × 1000)` (accurate to the enqueue column's one-second resolution), `setup_ms` = `run_started_ms − accepted_ms` when the run reached the pipeline (includes the readiness wait), and `readiness_wait_ms` (the sum of the per-run gate's `ReadinessOutcome.waited_seconds` × 1000 for this execution).
3. THE Summary from the `engine` producer SHALL carry `detections: {runsWithDetections, total, perClass: {<label>: {total, max}}, truncated}` where `total` counts every detection of the Interval's completed runs **from each run's uncapped `detection_count`** (the run hook carries the full count beside its 1,000-item capped list, Requirement 5.2), `perClass.<label>.total` is the sum over runs of the capped lists, `perClass.<label>.max` is the largest per-run count, `perClass` holds at most 64 labels of at most 64 characters each, and `truncated` is true when labels were folded into `__other__` **or** when any run of the Interval carried more detections than its capped list, so that a run with more than 1,000 detections never undercounts `total` silently. Per-run means SHALL be computed by the Portal as `total / runs.completed`, never on the device.
4. WHERE a workflow's runs carry `is_anomalous`, THE Summary SHALL carry `outcomes: {anomalous, normal}`; otherwise `outcomes` SHALL be absent.
5. THE Summary from the `engine` producer SHALL carry `people: {seen}` as the per-run maximum of the detections whose label is in the person-class list the Portal writes into the Control_Shadow (`desired.personClasses`; **in this phase a fixed Portal constant `["person", "human"]`, not a setting**: the Portal writes it as-is into every Control_Shadow and the agent applies it with the same default when the key is absent, Requirement 6.1), when any such label occurred, and `model: {state, models: {<name>: <state>}, waits, waitedMs}` where each state is one of `NONE`, `READY`, `LOADING`, `UNLOADING`, `UNAVAILABLE`, `UNKNOWN`, `NOT_DEPLOYED`, `INCOMPLETE` (every state `model_gate.py` and `dda_triton.model_readiness` can report, plus `NONE`), `state` is the worst state seen in the Interval under the ordering `NONE < READY < UNLOADING < LOADING < INCOMPLETE < NOT_DEPLOYED < UNKNOWN < UNAVAILABLE`, `waits` counts readiness waits (per-run gate outcomes that were not immediately ready, plus continuous-runner waits that **began** in the Interval), and `waitedMs` is the sum of the per-run gate's `waited_seconds` × 1000 over the Interval (the time the Triton readiness gate held runs, which Requirement 2.2 excludes from `latency`). THE sources SHALL be two additive hooks: the per-run readiness gate (`_await_model_readiness`) reports each model's `ReadinessOutcome` (`state`, `waited_seconds`, `ready`) to the Telemetry_Agent, and the continuous runner reports each `ModelWait` document when a wait begins and when it ends. **Each continuous wait SHALL be counted exactly once**, from the `ModelWait` hook's None → non-None transition; the continuous runner's `modelUnavailable` counter, which `_note_model_wait` increments on the same transition, SHALL be ignored by the summarizer so that a wait is never counted twice (a unit test: one wait that polls three times yields `waits == 1`). A Pair whose registration's compiled document has no `emltriton` element (as the registration source of Requirement 2.8 reports it) SHALL report `model: {state: "NONE", models: {}, waits: 0, waitedMs: 0}`. An unrecognised state string SHALL be mapped to `UNKNOWN` on the device and never rejected by the ingestion. Continuous workflows SHALL add `continuous: {skippedNoNewFrame, paused, effectiveFps}`.
6. THE Summary MAY carry `ext: {<name>: {...}}`. THE `ppe` Extension SHALL be `ext.ppe: {people: {seen, compliant, violating}, violations: {frames, byReason: {<reason>: {frames, objects}}}, clipsOpened, frames}`, where `compliant + violating ≤ seen`, reasons are at most 64 characters and at most 16 per message. An Extension SHALL be owned by one producer name: THE ingestion SHALL fold `runs`, `latency`, `detections`, `outcomes`, `people.seen`, `model` and `continuous` only from the `engine` producer, and `ext.*` from the producer that owns the extension; blocks a producer does not own SHALL be ignored and counted `block_not_owned`.
7. THE Summary SHALL carry `telemetry: {queueDrops, publishFailures, eventsDropped}`: counters of the Telemetry_Agent itself since the previous Summary.
8. THE set of Pairs the Telemetry_Agent reports SHALL be **every active registration of the Device** (`WorkflowRegistration` rows in status `registered`), read from one registration source shared with the debug mode (`telemetry/registry_view.py`, which subscribes to the engine's `WorkflowWatcher.registrations_listeners` hook as the trigger runtime and the continuous manager do, and reads the rows and each registration's compiled document once); a Pair SHALL be tracked from the moment its registration appears (without waiting for a first run) and SHALL stop being reported, after a final Summary or Heartbeat for the window in progress, when its registration leaves `registered` (`superseded` or `removed`), so that a deployed-but-never-triggered workflow reads `idle` rather than `silent` and a removed workflow does not heartbeat until the next LocalServer restart. THE Telemetry_Agent SHALL publish one Summary per Pair per Interval while the Pair had at least one run in the last 5 minutes, at the Interval's end plus at most 1 s, with QoS 0, carrying the registration's `version` as `workflowVersion` (a version change inside an Interval yields one Summary per version for that Interval).
9. WHILE a Pair has had no run for 5 minutes, or has never run since its registration appeared, THE Telemetry_Agent SHALL publish a Heartbeat every `idleHeartbeatS` (default 60, range 30–300) instead of per-Interval Summaries. A Heartbeat SHALL declare its own window: `intervalS = idleHeartbeatS`, `intervalEnd` aligned to a multiple of `idleHeartbeatS`, `intervalStart = intervalEnd − idleHeartbeatS × 1000` (a short first window is allowed, as for a Summary), and `idle: true`, so that the length rule of Requirement 2.1 accepts it; a Heartbeat that declares `intervalS = summaryIntervalS` over a longer window SHALL be rejected `schema_invalid` (the schema tests include a 60 s Heartbeat from a 10 s producer, accepted, and a Heartbeat declaring `intervalS: 10` over 60 s, rejected).
10. THE Schema_Registry SHALL define every field above with its type and limits, and THE ingestion SHALL reject a Summary whose required fields are missing or invalid as `schema_invalid`, SHALL coerce declared optional fields of the wrong type as `output_bindings._coerce` does (numeric strings to numbers, `"true"`/`"false"` to booleans) and drop those that still fail (counted `FieldDropped`), and SHALL keep undeclared fields in the lake record only.

### Requirement 3: The event contract, `dda.event.v1`

**User Story:** As a dashboard user, I want violations, clips and run errors to appear within seconds of happening, so that the dashboard responds to what matters rather than to a summary cadence.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. THE Event SHALL be a JSON object of at most 16 KB with `schema: "dda.event.v1"`, `type` ∈ {`violation_confirmed`, `clip_ready`, `run_error`}, `eventId` (UUID), `ts` (epoch milliseconds), `deviceId`, `workflowId`, `workflowVersion`, `producer: {name, version, instance}`, optional `executionId`, `severity` ∈ {`info`, `warning`, `error`} and a `data` object whose shape depends on `type`.
2. A Producer SHALL publish an Event immediately when it occurs, with QoS 1, through the same bounded queue as Summaries. THE Telemetry_Agent SHALL publish at most 60 Events per minute per Device; Events beyond that SHALL be dropped and counted in the next Summary's `telemetry.eventsDropped`.
3. `run_error.data` SHALL be `{error (at most 1,024 characters, redacted), failingNodeId, count, firstTs, lastTs}`. THE Telemetry_Agent SHALL coalesce run errors per (Workflow, `failingNodeId`, first 64 characters of the error) so that at most one `run_error` Event per such key is published every 10 s, carrying the `count` of coalesced failures. "Redacted" means passed through the vendored `workflow_engine.vendor.workflow_core.stream_url.redact(text, secrets)` with `secrets = dda_logging.redaction.credential_store_secrets()` (the string function; `dda_logging.redaction.RedactingFilter` is the `logging.Filter` built on the same rules and is not a string API). THE ingestion's second pass SHALL use the same function under its Portal name, `workflow_core.stream_url.redact`, which exists on the Portal **only in the `workflow_core` Lambda layer** (`backend/layers/workflow_core/python/workflow_core/stream_url.py`; the shared layer has no equivalent); THE new stack SHALL therefore attach that layer to the ingest function (Requirement 17.6), THE ingest SHALL import `redact` only through the one-line module `backend/functions/telemetry/redaction.py` (`from workflow_core.stream_url import redact`), and a unit test SHALL import **that module** with the layer **absent** from `sys.path` and assert the import fails, so that the dependency is pinned rather than masked by the test harness, which puts the layer on `sys.path` for every test (`backend/tests/conftest.py`); `telemetry_ingest.py` itself imports boto3 and `shared_utils` and cannot serve as the probe. THE difference in strength between this redaction and the debug mode's `redact_structure` (which adds header values, connection-string keys and AWS key patterns) is **accepted and recorded**: the Event texts are engine exception messages, not log lines or request dumps, and the base rules mask the secrets such a message can quote; the design records the reason and the one-line path to widen it later.
4. `clip_ready.data` SHALL be `{clipId (at most 128 characters, `[A-Za-z0-9._:-]`), revision (integer ≥ 1), startTs, endTs (endTs ≥ startTs, at most 3,600 s apart), durationS, reasons: {<reason>: {frames, objects}}, totals: {perClass, violationFrames, violationObjects}, file: {name, bytes, sha256, width, height, codec}, s3: {bucket, key, sidecarKey} or null, s3Error}`. `s3Error` (at most 200 characters) is rendered in the Clip_Gallery and SHALL be redacted by the Producer before publishing and again by the ingestion before storage, with the same rules as Requirement 3.3 (an S3 error text can quote a presigned URL or a request header). THE ingestion SHALL key a Clip_Index entry by (use case, Workflow, Device, `startTs`, `clipId`); a higher `revision` SHALL replace the media fields, a lower or equal one SHALL change nothing, and counts SHALL count each clip once. **A `clip_ready` revision of the same clip SHALL carry the identical `clipId`, `startTs` and `deviceId` as its first publish** (the Producer's obligation, stated in the helper contract of Requirement 13.3); because the entry is keyed on them, a follow-up with a different `startTs` is a new clip, not a revision.
5. `violation_confirmed.data` SHALL be `{reasons: {<reason>: objects}, people, violatingPeople, clipId or null}`.
6. THE ingestion SHALL reject an Event whose required fields are missing or invalid as `schema_invalid`, and SHALL de-duplicate Events by `eventId` within 24 hours.

### Requirement 4: Topic convention

**User Story:** As a workflow author, I want one documented topic namespace for dashboard telemetry, so that a new workflow shows up on dashboards without per-workflow IoT setup and never collides with trigger or legacy topics.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. THE Convention_Topic SHALL be `dda/telemetry/{usecase_id}/{workflow_id}/{kind}` with kind ∈ {`summary`, `event`, `live`}, where `usecase_id` and `workflow_id` are the Portal's UUIDs. THE root SHALL be the two literal levels `dda/telemetry`, which no existing DDA feature, test fixture or rule uses; THE design SHALL re-confirm this in the worktree.
2. THE convention SHALL NOT carry a device level: the Device SHALL come from Attribution (Requirement 7). THE payload's `deviceId` SHALL be a cross-check.
3. THE Telemetry_Agent SHALL take the use case id from the Control_Shadow (`desired.usecaseId`), never from the packaged manifest, which carries none. WHILE the Control_Shadow carries no use case id, THE Telemetry_Agent SHALL publish nothing for that Device.
4. THE IoT rules SHALL match the exact five-level filter `dda/telemetry/+/+/+` and nothing wider. No rule of this feature SHALL use `#`, and no rule SHALL match `ppe-demo/mic730/invoke` or any other trigger topic.
5. THE design SHALL show how the existing `mqtt_publish` output continues unchanged beside the convention: the node's topics and payloads are not ingested by this feature and need no change; a workflow needs no `mqtt_publish` node to appear on dashboards.
6. WHEN `POST /workflows/{id}/duplicate` copies a workflow, THE copy's `mqtt_publish` topics SHALL be unaffected by this feature (they are not Convention_Topics); THE design SHALL record that the summaries of the copy are attributed to the copy's own workflow id because the Telemetry_Agent takes the id from the registration, not from any node.

### Requirement 5: Device-side producer through Greengrass IPC

**User Story:** As the owner, I want every deployed workflow to publish summaries and events from LocalServer itself through Greengrass IPC with component access control, so that no workflow change, no per-workflow deployment and no broad device policy is needed.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. THE design SHALL choose the executor-level emitter over a summary mode on `mqtt_publish` and over a new output node, and SHALL state the reasons (failed and dropped runs are visible only to the engine; a node would need every workflow re-packaged and re-deployed; summarizing across runs needs state that outlives a run).
2. THE Telemetry_Agent SHALL observe every run through a hook on `WorkflowExecutor` that receives one `RunRecord` per run with: the registration id, workflow id and version, execution id, status, error, failing node id, `capture_id`, `output_dir`, `log_path`, the timing stamps of Requirement 2.2 (`enqueued_at_s`, `accepted_ms`, `run_started_ms` or `None`, `finished_ms`, `queued_ms`, `setup_ms` or `None`, `readiness_wait_ms`), the loaded trigger context (a dict), the node statuses, the run's final tag values, the detection list (at most 1,000 items) **and the uncapped `detection_count`**, the traceback of the failure when the failure was an exception (at most 8 KB; `None` for validation and preflight refusals) and the continuous-run flag; the model-readiness outcomes through the hooks of Requirement 2.5; the trigger gate's discards and the continuous runner's counters through hooks on those components; and a `CURRENT_EXECUTION` context variable `(registration_id, workflow_id, execution_id)` that the executor sets around `execute()`. THE executor SHALL bind every value the `finally` block passes to the hook before its `try` (`None` or `{}`), SHALL capture `enqueued_at_s` and `accepted_ms` right after the pending check and before the RUNNING transition overwrites `started_at`, SHALL stamp `run_started_ms` at the RUNNING transition, SHALL call the hook only when the run's registration was resolved, SHALL read the row's terminal fields (`status`, `error`, `failing_node_id`, `capture_id`, `output_dir`, `log_path`) in the `finally` through a **fresh** session (`self._session_factory()`) guarded by `try/except` with the in-memory row as the fallback, because the run's own session may be the thing that broke (`_mark_failed_best_effort` uses a fresh session for the same reason), and SHALL never let the hook raise into the run. THE hook SHALL enqueue and return within 2 ms at p95 for a run with 1,000 detections (unit-tested benchmark); a unit test SHALL assert that a run enqueued 3 s before `execute()` reports `queued_ms` ≈ 3,000 after completing, and that a run held 2 s by a stubbed readiness gate reports `readiness_wait_ms` ≈ 2,000 and a latency that excludes it. THE `RunRecord` and the hook module SHALL be defined once, in this specification's design, and used unchanged by the debug mode; so SHALL the registration source of Requirement 2.8 (`registry_view.py`), which the debug mode uses to decide whether a session's Workflow has a registration on the Device.
3. THE Telemetry_Agent SHALL publish through the shared IPC client (`utils/ipc_client.py`) with `PublishToIoTCore` through **`publish_via_ipc(topic, payload: bytes, qos, *, retain: bool = False)`**, factored out of `output_bindings._default_greengrass_publisher` so that the **whole** IPC body moves into it — the QoS mapping, the optional `retain` assignment with its SDK-field check, `call_with_ipc_retry` and the nucleus-denial diagnosis including its `iot:RetainPublish` sentence — and `_default_greengrass_publisher` (which keeps its `(topic, payload: str, qos, *, retain)` signature) becomes a one-line delegate that encodes the payload and passes `retain=retain`; BECAUSE the retained path is live and pinned by `test/backend-test/workflow_engine/test_mqtt_publish_retain.py`, THAT test SHALL stay green **unchanged**, and the agents SHALL call `publish_via_ipc` with the default `retain=False`; from a single publisher thread fed by a bounded queue of 1,000 messages that drops the oldest message when full and counts the drop (Requirement 2.7). THE Telemetry_Agent and the debug mode's agent SHALL each own their **own** `IpcPublisher` instance (thread and queue), so that debug traffic can never evict Summaries from the agents' own queues (the nucleus's shared spooler, which that isolation does not reach, is bounded by the debug mode's byte cap, below); the publisher SHALL also expose `publish_message(topic, payload: str)` (QoS 0) so that `mqtt.SubscriptionHandler` can use it as its `publish_handler` for the shadow `get`. BECAUSE every component's `PublishToIoTCore` shares the nucleus's one in-memory MQTT spooler (`mqtt.spooler.maxSizeInBytes`, 2.5 MB by default, which rejects new messages when full), THE publisher thread SHALL recognise a spool-full rejection, count it as `publishFailures` with `reason: spool_full` and log it once per minute per topic naming the shared spooler; the byte cap that keeps the debug mode from filling that spooler is the debug mode specification's (its Requirement 7.1).
4. THE design SHALL spell out the recipe and access-control statements the agent relies on: `mqttproxy:2` (`PublishToIoTCore` on `*`) for the Convention_Topics and `mqttproxy:1` (`PublishToIoTCore` and `SubscribeToIoTCore` on `$aws/things/*/shadow/name/*`) for the Control_Shadow topics. Both are present in all seven recipes (`recipe.yaml`, `recipe-amd64.yaml`, `recipe-amd64-nvidia.yaml`, `recipe-arm64.yaml`, `recipe-arm64-jp5.yaml`, `recipe-arm64-jp6.yaml` and `recipe-arm64-jp7.yaml`), so no recipe change and therefore no access-control deployment is needed for this feature (resolved: the `amd64Nvidia` recipe carries `mqttproxy:2` since the trunk integration, device verification pending with the first `amd64Nvidia` build; before that it carried `mqttproxy:1` only and the variant was unsupported, A10/Q8). BECAUSE `_default_greengrass_publisher`'s existing denial diagnosis (`output_bindings.py` ~771–783, re-raised as a `RuntimeError` whose `__cause__` is the `UnauthorizedError`) names four recipes generically and never `mqttproxy:2`, `recipe-amd64-nvidia.yaml` or `recipe-arm64-jp7.yaml`, and BECAUSE the device has no way to know which variant it runs, THE agent's publisher thread SHALL, when the failure is such a denial, log once per minute per topic **its own fixed line** that names the missing statement — `publish to <topic> denied by Greengrass IPC: this LocalServer's deployed recipe lacks mqttproxy:2 (aws.greengrass#PublishToIoTCore on *). Original diagnosis: <message>` (no variant is named: since A10/Q8 were resolved every current recipe carries the statement) — leaving `publish_via_ipc` and the original diagnosis unchanged (its existing tests pin them); a unit test SHALL stub `publish_via_ipc` to raise the `RuntimeError`-from-`UnauthorizedError` and assert the line, the once-per-minute cadence and the `publishFailures` count. A recipe test SHALL assert that every `recipe*.yaml` at the repository root except a named exception list (empty since the trunk integration; it named `recipe-amd64-nvidia.yaml` before A10/Q8 were resolved) carries `mqttproxy:2` with `aws.greengrass#PublishToIoTCore` on `*` and `mqttproxy:1` on `$aws/things/*/shadow/name/*`, so that the claim is pinned and any exception is explicit. THE design SHALL state that if a later recipe narrows `mqttproxy:2`, it must keep `dda/telemetry/*`.
5. THE Telemetry_Agent SHALL NOT depend on the hand-attached token-exchange policy. Its only AWS SDK calls SHALL be S3 `PutObject` of live frames under the Media_Location, authorized by the Device_Role's `AllowInferenceResultsUpload` statement (or the override bucket's `UseCase<id>Results` statement).
6. THE Telemetry_Agent SHALL read the Control_Shadow over MQTT through IPC: subscribe to the prefix `$aws/things/{thing}/shadow/name/dda-workflow-telemetry/#` (the thing name from `AWS_IOT_THING_NAME`, as the existing agents read it), publish an empty `.../get` each time the subscription becomes active, and apply the **complete** `desired` document from `get/accepted` (`state.desired`, `version`) and from `update/documents` (`current.state.desired`, `current.version`), in version order; an `update/delta` SHALL only trigger a fresh `.../get` (a delta is partial and never carries a removed key, so it is never applied as the desired state). BECAUSE the `#` subscription also delivers the agent's own request publishes, messages on `.../get`, `.../update`, `.../update/accepted`, `.../update/rejected` and `.../get/rejected` SHALL be ignored (a DEBUG line each; a unit test asserts that an empty `.../get` echo changes nothing). An entry absent from the complete document (a `live.<workflowId>` lease the Portal removed by writing `null`) SHALL be treated as removed. It SHALL apply `usecaseId`, `enabled`, `summaryIntervalS`, `idleHeartbeatS` and `live.<workflowId>`, and SHALL write `reported` (`agentVersion`, the applied values, `appliedVersion`, `lastPublishAt`, counters) through `.../update` when a non-counter field changes and otherwise at most once per 5 minutes. It SHALL NOT depend on the ShadowManager `synchronize` list, because extending that list needs a deployment. THE design SHALL state that this MQTT `get` → `get/accepted` read is new code (the existing agents read the document through ShadowManager IPC) and that RB-7 verifies the round trip.
7. WHILE `desired.enabled` is not true, THE Telemetry_Agent SHALL publish nothing. WHEN `desired.enabled` becomes true, THE agent SHALL start within one Interval with **every accumulator reset** and the first Interval starting at the enable time (runs observed while disabled are not folded into the first Summary), and WHEN it becomes false, SHALL stop within one Interval, discarding the Interval in progress, with no restart and no deployment.
8. THE Telemetry_Agent SHALL start on a daemon thread behind the same exception guards as the camera-registry and user-accounts agents, so that a failure of the agent never affects LocalServer startup or any run.
9. Edge changes SHALL follow `.kiro/steering/builds.md`: unit-tested in this phase, built and verified on a device of each touched architecture before they are committed, and never deployed while another build runs.

### Requirement 6: Use-case telemetry enablement and settings

**User Story:** As a UseCaseAdmin, I want to turn dashboards on for my use case in one place, see what is still missing, and set the summary cadence, so that I know whether data will flow and what it costs.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. THE Portal SHALL keep Telemetry_Settings per use case: `enabled` (default false), `summaryIntervalS` (default 10, range 5–60), `idleHeartbeatS` (default 60, range 30–300), the derived Media_Location (bucket, prefix, source, the bucket's `region` and its `region_source`, Requirement 10.2), and when and by whom they last changed. **`personClasses` is not a Telemetry_Setting in this phase:** the Portal writes the fixed constant `PERSON_CLASSES = ["person", "human"]` (`backend/functions/telemetry/constants.py`) into every Control_Shadow it writes; `PUT …/settings` SHALL refuse a `personClasses` key with 400 `invalid_settings`, so that making it configurable later is an additive change.
2. WHEN `PUT /telemetry/usecases/{usecase_id}/settings` changes `enabled` or an interval, THE Portal SHALL write the Control_Shadow of every Device that has a Device_Record for the use case (`usecase_devices(usecase)`: a filtered `Scan` of the Devices table, never the `usecase-devices-index`) with `desired.usecaseId`, `desired.enabled`, `desired.summaryIntervalS`, `desired.idleHeartbeatS`, SHALL report per Device whether the write succeeded, listing in `unrecorded_devices` the managed core devices the Portal lists for the use case that have no Device_Record; above `SHADOW_WRITE_SYNC_LIMIT` (40) Devices THE Portal SHALL answer 202 and the maintenance SHALL finish the writes within 5 minutes; and SHALL audit `telemetry_enabled`, `telemetry_disabled` or `telemetry_settings_changed` with the actor and the use case.
3. WHEN a Device is added to a Dashboard, THE Portal SHALL write that Device's Control_Shadow with the use case's current settings at once; and every 15 minutes THE maintenance SHALL write the Control_Shadow of every Device_Record of an enabled use case whose shadow write is missing, failed or stale, so that a Device recorded after the settings were written publishes within 15 minutes. (The Portal's device listing has no hook and is not changed, Requirement 20.1.)
4. WHEN a holder of `telemetry:manage` opens the settings, THE Portal SHALL show a readiness checklist with a remedy per missing item: the ingestion resources for the use case's account (the rule and its error role are present; for the `cross` Topology, the use-case account template carries the telemetry update); region support; **device records** (every managed core device the Portal lists for the use case has a Device_Record naming it; the item lists the devices that do not, with the Device_Record_Remedy); at least one Summary seen for the use case in 24 h; the Media_Location is reachable (Requirement 14.4); the media lifecycle rules are in place (Requirement 14.5); registry enforcement (a warning, Requirement 17.5).
5. WHERE a use case's region differs from the Portal's region, THE Portal SHALL refuse `enabled: true` with 400 `region_not_supported` and the checklist SHALL say so.
6. WHILE telemetry is disabled for a use case, THE ingestion SHALL store nothing for it and SHALL count its messages as `usecase_disabled`; its Dashboards SHALL say that telemetry is off; stored data SHALL age out under Requirement 8.
7. WHEN a use case is deleted, THE Portal SHALL delete its Dashboards and Telemetry_Settings, SHALL write `desired.enabled: false` to its Devices' Control_Shadows on a best-effort basis, and SHALL stop attributing messages to it within 15 minutes.

### Requirement 7: Ingestion and attribution

**User Story:** As a Portal operator, I want every telemetry message validated and attributed to exactly one Device and use case before it is stored, so that no device can write into another use case's dashboards and no unvalidated data reaches the stores.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. THE IoT rules SHALL be `dda_tel_ingest` (one per account that has Devices: the Portal account's copy in the Portal stacks under a distinct name, the use-case account's copy in `usecase-account-stack.ts` gated like the camera-shadow rule), with filter `dda/telemetry/+/+/+`, SQL version `2016-03-23`, `SELECT encode(*, 'base64') AS p, topic() AS t, principal() AS pr, clientid() AS c, timestamp() AS r, newuuid() AS m`, one **Lambda action** that invokes the Portal account's ingest function through a per-account alias, and a CloudWatch Logs error action in the rule's own account.
2. THE design SHALL justify the Lambda action over a direct DynamoDB or Firehose action (validation, attribution, cross-account delivery with a tamper-proof account) and over an SQS hop (no per-message queue cost, asynchronous invocation with Lambda's own retries and an on-failure destination).
3. THE ingest function SHALL take the sending account from the alias it was invoked through (`context.invoked_function_arn`), never from the rule SQL or the payload. Each alias's resource-based policy SHALL allow `lambda:InvokeFunction` only to `iot.amazonaws.com` with `aws:SourceAccount` equal to that alias's account and `aws:SourceArn` like `arn:aws:iot:<region>:<account>:rule/dda_tel_*`.
4. FOR each message, THE ingestion SHALL: decode `p` as a JSON object of at most 128 KB (else `invalid_json` or `too_large`); parse `t` as a Convention_Topic (else `attribution_mismatch`; unknown kind `schema_unknown`); resolve the Device from `pr` through `ListPrincipalThings` in the sending account (cached 1 h; a negative result 5 min) and require that the thing equals `c` with any `#<n>` suffix removed (else `attribution_mismatch`); require that the Device's Device_Record names a use case whose `account_id` is the sending account and whose id equals the topic's use case (else `attribution_mismatch`; this is fail-closed on purpose: the Device_Record is the only binding of a thing to one use case, and the readiness item of Requirement 6.4 makes a missing record visible); require that the Workflow belongs to that use case (else `workflow_not_in_usecase`); require that telemetry is enabled (else `usecase_disabled`); validate the payload against the Schema_Registry (else `schema_invalid`); require that the payload's `deviceId` and `workflowId`, when present, match (else `attribution_mismatch`); and, for Events only, drop as `rate_exceeded` an Event for a Pair that has already accepted 120 Events in the current minute (twice the agent's cap of Requirement 3.2, counted per container in a sliding window), so that a Helper outside the agent's cap cannot flood the Event_Store.
5. THE ingestion SHALL store a Summary idempotently under (`receivedMs`, `messageId`), so that a Lambda retry of the same event overwrites itself; a device-side retransmission of a Summary (the same `instance` and `seq` under a new `messageId`, which only an IoT double delivery of a QoS 0 publish produces) SHALL be **stored**, counted `duplicate` (a diagnostic reason; the message is accepted) when its `seq` is not above the `seq` of the `LAST#`/`LASTHB#` pointer for the same `instance` (the same pointer comparison the gap estimate below performs), and folded **once** by the roll-up, which de-duplicates on (producer, `instance`, `seq`) in memory (Requirement 8.3); the ingestion SHALL NOT reject a Summary as a duplicate. Events SHALL be stored under `eventId` (the Event_Store key carries it), so that a QoS 1 redelivery overwrites itself. Gaps (a missing `seq` within an `instance`) SHALL be detected in two places with two roles: the ingestion SHALL record a **per-message estimate** (`gap = seq − last − 1` on the Summary_Store item that follows the gap, and the `Gaps` metric), which is an estimate because asynchronous Lambda invocations do not preserve order (a delayed retry processed after its successor yields one false gap and one lower-`seq` message stored without correction); and THE roll-up SHALL compute the **authoritative** `gaps` measure of each minute item from the stored (producer, `instance`, `seq`) values of its query window, which sees the whole set (Requirement 8.3). THE Health_Panel SHALL show the roll-up's `gaps` (Requirement 11.2), never the per-message estimate.
6. ### Requirement 8: Storage, aggregation and retention

**User Story:** As a dashboard user, I want seconds-fresh latest values and minute and hour charts over months, so that the Now panel is live and the charts stay cheap.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. THE Summary_Store SHALL hold one item per accepted Summary keyed by Pair and receive time, with the full validated Summary, `receivedMs`, `eventMs`, `gap` and `messageId`, with a TTL of 48 h. A Summary's `eventMs` SHALL be `intervalEnd` when it lies within **5 minutes before and 5 minutes after** the receive time `r`, and otherwise the receive time flagged `tsSource: received`, so that every accepted Summary's receive-time key lies inside the roll-up's query window for its `eventMs` minute (Requirement 8.3) and a device clock more than 5 minutes off still charts (a property test asserts it for every accepted Summary). An Event's `eventMs` SHALL be its `ts` when within 24 h before and 5 minutes after `r` (the nucleus spools QoS 1 Events while a device is offline, up to its 2.5 MB in-memory spooler, so an Event can legitimately arrive hours late and must chart at its real time), else the receive time flagged `tsSource: received`; the Event_Store SHALL key Events by `eventMs`, so the roll-up's minute query over Events is self-consistent. Heartbeats SHALL be stored in the Summary_Store only. THE Summary_Store SHALL also hold, per Pair, two pointer items per producer, so that an idle line keeps its last values: `LAST#<producer>`, updated only by non-Heartbeat Summaries (the newest values, `receivedMs`, `eventMs`, and `lastRunMs`: the `receivedMs` of the newest Summary whose `runs.completed + runs.failed > 0`, which the Now_Panel's "last run <age>" reads, Requirement 11.1), and `LASTHB#<producer>`, updated only by Heartbeats (`receivedMs`), plus one live-frame pointer item (Requirement 10).
2. THE Aggregate_Store SHALL hold one item per (Pair, minute) for 7 days, one per (Pair, hour) for 400 days and **one per (Pair, UTC day) for 400 days**, each carrying the folded measures of the Measure_Catalog (sums, maxima, run counts, per-class totals, per-reason totals, event counts by type; latency p50 folded as the run-count-weighted mean of the Summaries' p50 and labelled approximate, p95 as the maximum of the Summaries' p95, and the exact maximum), the number of Summaries folded and the roll-up's `gaps` (Requirement 7.5). The same fold rule SHALL apply from Summaries to minutes, from minutes to hours and from hours to days, so that an absolute range of 400 days is 400 day items per Device (Requirement 9.4) while hour items keep the 60-day charts and the lake add-on's reconciliation (Requirement 16).
3. THE minute items SHALL be computed by a roll-up function fed by the Summary_Store's DynamoDB stream (batch window 60 s), which recomputes each affected (Pair, minute) from the Summary_Store (a `Query` over the receive-time keys from the minute's start minus 5 minutes to its end plus 5 minutes, keeping the items whose `eventMs` falls in the minute: the same ±5-minute bound as Requirement 8.1's `eventMs` rule, so no accepted Summary can fall outside the query) and overwrites the item, so that a retried batch is idempotent; the hour item of each affected hour SHALL be recomputed from its minute items, and the day item of each affected UTC day from its hour items, in the same roll-up invocation. THE roll-up SHALL compute the minute's `gaps` from the (producer, `instance`, `seq`) values of the Summaries in its query window: for each `instance`, the sum of the holes between consecutive stored `seq` values whose later member's `eventMs` falls in the minute. A minute item SHALL be final within 2 minutes of its minute closing at p95.
4. Aggregates SHALL bucket a Summary by the minute of **`eventMs − 1`** (a Summary's `eventMs` is its `intervalEnd`, and with wall-clock-aligned Intervals the Summary covering :50–:00 ends exactly on the minute boundary; `eventMs − 1` puts it in the minute it covers instead of the next one), keeping `eventMs` as stored, and SHALL bucket an Event by the minute of its `eventMs`; the Now_Panel and Health_Panel SHALL compute flow state and age from `receivedMs`, and SHALL show `eventMs` beside it.
5. THE Event_Store SHALL hold every accepted Event for 30 days, keyed by Pair and `eventMs` with the `by-workflow` index (`W#<uc>#<wf>` / `<eventMs13>#<device>#<eventId>`) that the events route queries by time range, applying `type` and `deviceId` as a `FilterExpression` (there is no type index), and the Clip_Index entries for `telemetryClipRetentionDays` (default 90, range 7–365).
6. THE Lake SHALL receive every accepted Summary (not Heartbeats) and Event as one JSON line through two Data Firehose streams (`summaries`, `events`), GZIP, buffered up to 300 s or 64 MB, under `raw/summaries/dt=YYYY-MM-DD/` and `raw/events/dt=YYYY-MM-DD/` in a Portal-owned bucket whose name does not start with `dda-` and does not contain `-dda-`, with a 400-day lifecycle (`telemetryLakeRetentionDays`), SSE-S3, Block Public Access, TLS-only and no versioning. No Glue table, Athena workgroup or Quick Sight asset SHALL be created in this phase.
7. Every DynamoDB read SHALL filter expired items (`expires_at > now`), because TTL deletion lags.
8. THE design SHALL state the retention of every store in one table, with its mechanism.

### Requirement 10: The Live_Tile

**User Story:** As a dashboard user, I want to see the latest annotated frame of a device while I watch, so that I can see what the workflow detects as it runs.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. THE Live_Tile SHALL show the latest annotated frame of one selected Device of the Dashboard, with its capture time, age, the detections' per-class counts and, for the `ppe` Extension, the violation state. The Producer SHALL draw the boxes and labels; the Portal SHALL not overlay.
2. THE Telemetry_Agent SHALL send Live_Frames for a Pair only while a Live_Lease for it is in the Control_Shadow and unexpired: after each completed run, at most `maxFps` (default 1, at most 2) per second, it SHALL downscale the run's overlay JPEG (or the raw capture) to at most `maxWidth` × `maxHeight` (1280 × 720), re-encode at JPEG quality 80 and then 60 until the frame is within `maxBytes` (307,200), **skip and count (`framesTooLarge`) a frame still above `maxBytes` after the second re-encode** (the API's HEAD check would refuse it anyway, Requirement 10.4), PUT it to `dda-telemetry/live/<usecase_id>/<device>/<workflow_id>/<slot>.jpg` where `slot = seq mod 16`, with `Content-Type: image/jpeg` and user metadata `x-amz-meta-seq`, and only then publish the `dda.live.v1` message (`ts`, `instance` (the agent's UUID, the same as the Summaries' `producer.instance`), `seq` (per `instance`, restarting at 0 with the agent), `deviceId`, `workflowId`, `frame: {bucket, key, width, height, bytes}`, `perClass`, `people`, `violation`, `reasons`) with QoS 0. THE live-frame pointer SHALL be updated in **arrival order** (`receivedMs`), never by comparing `seq` across instances, so that the tile recovers from a LocalServer or agent restart on the next poll. The lease entry SHALL carry the bucket's `region` so the agent's S3 client needs no `GetBucketLocation` call and never meets `PermanentRedirect`. BECAUSE **nothing in the repository creates the default Results_Bucket** (`usecase-account-stack.ts` imports only `aws-iam`, `aws-events`, `aws-events-targets` and `aws-iot` and defines no `s3.Bucket` or `CfnBucket`; the name `dda-inference-results-*` appears there only in IAM resource ARNs; `deployments.py` only writes the name into the InferenceUploader configuration; the InferenceUploader artifacts contain no `create_bucket`; the banner in `deploy-account-role.sh` that says the stack creates the bucket is stale and is noted in the report, not fixed here), THE default bucket is operator-created (RB-3) and may be in any region, so THE Portal SHALL take `media_location.region` for **every** Results_Bucket, default or override, from the `x-amz-bucket-region` response header of the "Media location" readiness probe's `ListObjectsV2` response (Requirement 14.4), SHALL fall back to the use case's region only when the header is absent, and SHALL record which source it used in `media_location.region_source` (`header` | `usecase`) on the Telemetry_Settings; THE "Media location" readiness item SHALL show a warning when the header's region differs from the use case's region ("devices PUT across regions: transfer cost and latency"), and the lease SHALL still be granted. It SHALL stop within 10 s of the lease lapsing, whether `expiresAt` passed or the entry disappeared from the complete `desired` document (Requirement 5.6).
3. `POST /telemetry/dashboards/{id}/live-lease {device_id}` SHALL acquire or renew the caller's lease (one per viewer per Dashboard; `telemetryLiveMaxLeases` slots per use case, default 5, range 1–20, the slot items `SLOT#0` … `SLOT#<telemetryLiveMaxLeases − 1>` read from the deployment value, never a hard-coded five; 60 s lifetime renewed every 20 s while the tile is visible), SHALL write `desired.live.<workflow_id> = {expiresAt, maxFps, maxWidth, maxHeight, maxBytes, bucket}` to the Device's Control_Shadow with the latest expiry among the use case's leases for that Pair, and SHALL answer 429 `live_capacity_reached` with `reason: usecase_slots` when every slot is held, 429 `live_capacity_reached` with `reason: device_live_limit` when the Device already carries leases for 8 other Workflows (the agent keeps at most 8 `live` entries, Requirement 15.3, so the Portal SHALL never write a ninth), 409 `live_viewing_limit` after 30 continuous minutes (`telemetryLiveMaxViewMinutes`), 409 `telemetry_disabled` when telemetry is off, 409 `live_unavailable` with a reason when frames cannot be expired (Requirement 14.5), and 502 `live_lease_failed` when the shadow write fails.
4. `GET /telemetry/dashboards/{id}/live?device_id=&have=` SHALL take as `have` the opaque `frame_token` (`<instance>:<seq>`) its previous answer returned, SHALL mint a presigned GET valid at most 120 s only when the pointer's `frame_token` differs from `have` and a `HeadObject` shows `x-amz-meta-seq` equal to the pointer's `seq`, `ContentLength ≤ 307200` and `ContentType image/jpeg`, returning the new `frame_token` with it; otherwise it SHALL answer `frame: null` with the newest frame's age (echoing the token on a HEAD mismatch), and SHALL store no URL server-side. A unit test SHALL run two agent instances in sequence (`seq` 4000, then `seq` 0 arriving later) and assert that the pointer moves to the second and that the next poll receives a URL.
5. THE Live_Tile SHALL poll every 2 s while visible and not paused, SHALL show the states no live source, starting, live (frame ≤ 10 s old), stale, paused and unavailable (with the reason), and SHALL reach 5 s freshness at p95 at 1 frame/s.
6. THE Live_Tile SHALL never connect to a device-local server; the demo's loopback MJPEG view stays outside the product.

### Requirement 18: Security and least privilege
