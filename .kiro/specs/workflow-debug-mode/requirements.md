# Requirements Document

## Introduction

When a deployed workflow misbehaves on a device, the Portal today shows nothing about its runs, and the device-side evidence (per-run artifacts under `/aws_dda/captures/{workflowId}/{executionId}/`, `run.log`, `node_status_json`, the trigger context) is reachable only through the LocalServer UI on the device's network or over SSH. The owner's words (2026-10-15):

> "I would however, like a debug mode that sends all data viewable as a log somewhere when the workflow is misbehaving. It doesn't need to be part of the dashboards."

This specification adds a **workflow debug mode**: switched on for one (device, workflow), or for every device that runs a workflow, for a bounded time; while it is on, the device sends the **full record of every run** (not summaries) plus the workflow engine's log lines for that workflow to a log the owner can read in the Portal, with a console deep link for administrators. It is a **separate capability** from the `workflow-telemetry-dashboards` specification (an owner decision): it never feeds and never appears on the dashboards, and it works whether or not the dashboards ship. It is designed alongside them because it shares the device-side emitter infrastructure, the topic convention and the IoT and IAM plumbing.

**Facts that shape the requirements** (read in the worktree on 2026-10-15 and re-read on 2026-10-16):

- The executor (`workflow_engine/pipeline_executor.py`) persists per run: the `WorkflowExecution` row (`status`, `error`, `failing_node_id`, `started_at`, `finished_at`, `capture_id`, `output_dir`, `node_status_json`, `log_path`, `trigger_context_json`), the run metadata `{captureId}.json` (the final tag values, including the detection list and any Bedrock or LLM text), `{captureId}.jsonl`, `{captureId}.detections.json`, `run.log` (captured by `run_log.RunLogCapture` from the `workflow_engine` and `gstreamer` loggers **only** (`run_log.CAPTURED_LOGGERS`, ~56) at INFO and above, through `dda_logging.redaction`; the `dda_triton` logger, whose `model_readiness` module writes the readiness story at INFO and WARNING on the executor thread during `_await_model_readiness`, is **not** captured), and the overlay and base JPEGs. `RunRetention` prunes continuous runs only.
- Output bindings record their sent-message and skipped details into `node_status_json` through `detail_sink`, and their durations through `duration_sink`; model readiness comes from the per-run gate (`_await_model_readiness` calling `dda_triton.model_readiness.ensure_model_ready`, whose `ReadinessOutcome` carries `state`, `waited_seconds` and `ready` but is reduced to a message today; the gate waits up to `READY_TIMEOUT_S = 600` s for a loading model) and from the continuous runner's `model_gate.ModelGate` (`ModelWait` documents), with seven states (`READY`, `LOADING`, `UNLOADING`, `UNAVAILABLE`, `UNKNOWN`, `NOT_DEPLOYED`, `INCOMPLETE`); trigger firings are discarded in `trigger_runtime.TriggerGate.fire` without a counter; the continuous runner keeps `skippedBusy`, `skippedNoNewFrame` and `modelUnavailable` counters. The execution row's `started_at`/`finished_at` are integer seconds, and `started_at` is set at enqueue and **overwritten with the RUNNING time** (`pipeline_executor.py` ~1947–1948, after preflight, document load, frame-source staging and the readiness gate), so millisecond timing, and the enqueue time itself, must be captured by the executor as the dashboards specification's run hook defines (its Requirement 2.2: `enqueued_at_s`, `accepted_ms`, `run_started_ms` at the RUNNING transition, `finished_ms`, `queued_ms`, `setup_ms`, `readiness_wait_ms`).
- `run_log.RunLogCapture` attaches a `RotatingFileHandler` **at DEBUG level directly to the `workflow_engine` and `gstreamer` loggers** (not to the root) for the run's duration, so every record those loggers emit during a run, from any thread, lands in `run.log`; lowering a logger to DEBUG would therefore change `run.log` unless that handler filters DEBUG records out. `dda_logging.redaction.RedactingFilter` masks URL user information, secret query parameters (`password`, `token`, `secret`, …) and every Credential_Store value in log records; the string function behind it is the vendored `workflow_core.stream_url.redact(text, secrets)` with `dda_logging.redaction.credential_store_secrets()`; `dda_logging.run_context` quiets a continuous run's INFO and DEBUG lines from the component log while `RunLogCapture` keeps them. A `logging.Handler.emit` runs synchronously on the thread that logs, so anything a handler does per record is paid by the pipeline, GStreamer or binding thread that emitted it.
- The Portal's Devices table holds a Device_Record (`usecase_id`) only for devices that completed quick setup or were given attributes through `PUT /devices/{id}` (`devices.update_device_flags`: it needs `Permission.NODE_DESIGNER_MANAGE`, held by UseCaseAdmin and PortalAdmin only, requires `test_device` or `target_architecture` in the body, and is what the device page's "Target architecture" control calls); its `usecase-devices-index` is sparse and empty (its sort key `last_heartbeat` is never written). `workflow_tuning.registered_devices(usecase_id, workflow_id)` names the devices the Portal's Deployments table records as running a workflow, **but** it reads `DEPLOYMENTS_TABLE` from the environment (`workflow_tuning.py` ~151; the ComputeStack sets it) and importing `workflow_tuning` imports `workflow_core.anomaly_invocation`, `workflow_core.catalog.nodes`, `tuning_settings` and `workflows` at module load (~103–136), which live in the `workflow_core` layer and the ComputeStack's code asset.
- Two LocalServer agents (`camera_sync`, `user_accounts_sync`) already apply Portal-written named-shadow state without a deployment, through a `SubscriptionHandler` on `$aws/things/{thing}/shadow/name/{shadow}/update/` and a catch-up when the subscription becomes active; **their catch-up reads the document through ShadowManager IPC** (`IoTShadowAccessor.get_thing_shadow_state_request`), not through an MQTT `get`, so the `get` → `get/accepted` read this feature and the dashboards share is new code, verified on the device in the owner-gated phase. All seven LocalServer recipes authorize `PublishToIoTCore` on `*` (`mqttproxy:2`) and both shadow operations on `$aws/things/*/shadow/name/*` (`mqttproxy:1`). *(At the time of writing `recipe-amd64-nvidia.yaml` lacked `mqttproxy:2`, so the `amd64Nvidia` variant could not publish debug records and the dashboards specification's A10 and Q8 recorded it as unsupported; resolved: the `amd64Nvidia` recipe carries `mqttproxy:2` since the trunk integration, device verification pending with the first `amd64Nvidia` build; Q6.)* The Devices table records that hardware as `target_architecture = "x86_64_nvidia"` (`devices.py` `TARGET_ARCHITECTURES`); `amd64Nvidia` is the LocalServer component suffix (`build_domain.py`), never a Device_Record value, so every Portal-side check for the variant compares against `x86_64_nvidia`.
- The redaction function the ingest reuses, `workflow_core.stream_url.redact`, exists on the Portal **only in the `workflow_core` Lambda layer** (`backend/layers/workflow_core/python/workflow_core/stream_url.py`), which today only the ComputeStack, NodeDesigner and TestRunner stacks attach; the shared layer has no equivalent. The unit-test harness (`backend/tests/conftest.py`) puts that layer on `sys.path` for every test, so a function that forgot the layer would pass its tests and fail at import when deployed. The layer's jsonschema dependency carries a cp311-only native `rpds` module, so a Python 3.12 function may import only the stdlib-only `workflow_core.stream_url` from it.
- The Portal's existing permissions include `MANAGE_DEVICES` (the camera registry's mutation permission) and `VIEW_DEVICE_LOGS` (held by every role except DataLabeler); with `PORTAL_REGISTRY_ENFORCED` off (the trunk default) `_legacy_role` resolves every signed-in user to at least Viewer on every use case, and Viewer holds `VIEW_DEVICE_LOGS`, so in that mode every signed-in user can read every use case's device logs today. The Portal already reads CloudWatch Logs for its log-analysis feature (`logs:GetLogEvents`, `DescribeLogStreams`, `DescribeLogGroups`, `FilterLogEvents`).
- CloudWatch Logs accepts `PutLogEvents` batches of up to 1 MB and 10,000 events, each event up to 1 MB, timestamps within 14 days past and 2 hours future, without sequence tokens; its read APIs are bounded by **account-wide, non-adjustable** quotas (`GetLogEvents` 10 transactions per second in several Regions including us-west-2, eu-west-1 and eu-central-1, 25 elsewhere; `FilterLogEvents` 25 in us-east-1, 5 in several Regions, 10 elsewhere; `GetLogEvents` returns at most 10,000 events or 1 MB per call) that the Portal's Build Fleet, device-logs, plugin-build, git-sync and training log views already draw on; a log stream is **ordered by event timestamp**, and `GetLogEvents`'s `nextForwardToken` is a position in that order, so an event written later with an older timestamp is placed before a position a tail has already passed and a pure forward-token tail never shows it. The ingest path is asynchronous and at-least-once (IoT invokes Lambda asynchronously, which does not preserve order; a retried invocation can be delayed by minutes; several containers write one stream concurrently). AWS IoT messages are at most 128 KB; an IoT rule's CloudWatch Logs action picks the log group but not the log stream, and cannot cross accounts (only SQS, SNS, S3 and Lambda can).
- The Greengrass nucleus keeps **one** MQTT spooler for every component's `PublishToIoTCore`: an in-memory cache of `mqtt.spooler.maxSizeInBytes` = 2.5 MB by default that **rejects new messages when full** (QoS 1 messages are spooled while offline only until it is full; QoS 0 messages are discarded offline by default). A publisher that offers more bytes than the device's uplink carries fills it within seconds and then every component's publishes are rejected, including the dashboards' Summaries from the same device; a per-device cap must therefore be in bytes as well as messages.
- A Greengrass deployment restarts LocalServer and every model component and can wedge Triton until the `triton-model-cleanup-wedge` fix ships; switching debug on must therefore never need one.

**Three classes of work:** Portal changes (a CDK stack, Lambda handlers, the frontend, docs, tests), implemented and verified without deploying; edge changes (the Debug_Agent and its hooks), implemented and unit-tested but not built, deployed or committed until the owner-gated phase verifies them on a device (`.kiro/steering/builds.md`); account operations as Runbook_Tasks (deploy, template update, verification).

### Decisions and assumptions

- **D-A. Control through a named shadow**, `dda-workflow-debug`, read by the device over MQTT through IPC (not an MQTT command topic): the shadow persists the desired state while the device is offline, carries a version for ordering, gives the Portal a `reported` state to show, and is the pattern two agents already use. An MQTT command topic would need retained messages and a second channel for status.
- **D-B. Sink: CloudWatch Logs in the Portal account**, written by a Portal-account Lambda function that an IoT rule invokes (the only cross-account-capable path that lets the Portal name one log stream per (device, workflow)); one log group per use case, 14 days by default; a Portal "Debug log" page and a console deep link for PortalAdmins. **The CloudWatch event timestamp is the ingest's write time**, not the record's own `ts`, so the stream is ordered by arrival at the sink and a late or retried write always lands at the end where a tail finds it; the record's `ts` (device time) travels inside the JSON and orders the viewer's display. The viewer tails with a short overlapping time window plus `id` de-duplication, not with the forward token alone (Requirement 6).
- **D-C. Images off by default.** When on, one downscaled annotated JPEG per run, at most 2 per second and 2,000 per session, to the use case's Results_Bucket, 7-day lifecycle.
- **D-D. Caps.** 10 messages per second **and 256 KB per second** per device (the byte cap keeps a session from filling the Greengrass nucleus's MQTT spooler, a 2.5 MB in-memory cache shared by every component on the device that rejects new messages when full, which would silence the dashboards' Summaries from the same device); 120 KB per message; a 500-record drop-oldest queue; 4 concurrent sessions per use case (a session may cover several devices and counts once) and 2 per device; at most 20 devices per session; 60 minutes by default, 24 hours at most; on the read side, at most 4 `GetLogEvents` and 2 `FilterLogEvents` calls per second per API container (Requirement 6.3), fractions of the smallest Regional quota.
- **D-E. Permissions.** Enabling and disabling needs `MANAGE_DEVICES` (the Portal's device-mutation permission); reading needs `VIEW_DEVICE_LOGS`. No new permission and no new identity system. **Registry enforcement off:** the debug log stays readable under `VIEW_DEVICE_LOGS` exactly as the existing device-logs feature is (every signed-in user), with that exposure recorded for the owner gate (Q2); the **image** route, which can show people, is refused with 409 `registry_enforcement_required` while enforcement is off, the same guard the dashboards apply to clip playback and live frames (Requirement 3.4).
- **D-F. Independence.** The debug spec and the dashboards spec share four device-side modules (`hooks.py`, `publisher.py`, `control_shadow.py`, `registry_view.py`) and one Portal package (`edge_telemetry_common`: topics, identity, topology, devices, credentials, session policies, shadow pool). They are **defined once, in the dashboards design** (§5.2, §2.2, §2.3, §10.2, §14.4 there); this specification uses them unchanged and adds nothing to them except its own purposes in `session_policies.py`; when this specification lands first it creates `session_policies.py` with the four purposes it uses (`ingest` and `media-retention-read`, byte-identical to the dashboards' definitions, plus `debug-control` and `image-read`) and the dashboards later add theirs. Both specs land in one worktree, so each of these files exists **once**: whichever spec lands first creates it, the second imports it; there are **no fixture copies** and no byte-identity test for them (the dashboards' surface test over the three device modules is what catches an addition). The only files that genuinely exist twice are `topics.py` and this specification's `redact.py` (a device copy and a Portal copy each), which keep their two-copy identity tests. Each agent owns its **own** `IpcPublisher` instance (the dashboards' Requirement 5.3), so debug traffic never evicts or delays Summaries in the agents' own queues; the nucleus's shared MQTT spooler, which that isolation does not reach, is protected by the byte cap and the spool-full back-off of D-D and Requirement 7.1.
- **D-G. Log-line sources.** A run's log lines travel only inside that run's record: the INFO-and-above lines of the loggers `run.log` captures (`workflow_engine`, `gstreamer`) are read from `run.log`; every other line attributed to the run (the DEBUG lines of those two loggers, and **every** line of `dda_triton`, which `run.log` never captures) is buffered by the live tap and attached with `src: "tap"`. The tap sends a `log` record only for lines that belong to no run of the Pair. No line is sent twice. A line whose thread context (`CURRENT_EXECUTION` or `CONTINUOUS_RUN`) names a Pair **without** an active session is dropped and counted `otherPairLines`; the later attribution rules never see it, so another workflow's lines are never filed into the debugged session (Requirement 4.2). **The tap's `emit` does the minimum on the emitting thread** (capture the record's fields and the two context variables, enqueue, return); attribution by content, formatting beyond `getMessage()`, redaction and the size cap run on the agent's thread.

### Open questions carried to the owner gate

- **Q1.** The default session length (60 min) and the maximum (24 h).
- **Q2.** Whether `VIEW_DEVICE_LOGS` is the right read permission, given that run records can carry trigger payloads and LLM text, **and** that with registry enforcement off (the trunk default) every signed-in user holds it on every use case, so debug logs are then readable by every signed-in user, as the existing device-logs feature already is (images are refused in that mode, D-E).
- **Q3.** Whether PortalAdmins get the CloudWatch console deep link (it shows the account id of the Portal account to whoever follows it; its timestamp column is the sink's write time, with the record's own `ts` inside the JSON).
- **Q4.** Whether debug images (off by default) may be switched on for use cases whose cameras show people.
- **Q6.** **Closed — resolved: the `amd64Nvidia` recipe carries `mqttproxy:2` since the trunk integration (device verification pending with the first `amd64Nvidia` build); shared with the dashboards' Q8 and A10, closed the same way.** *Originally: the recipe lacked the statement and the variant was unsupported for debug mode until it gained it.*

### Out of scope

- Any display of debug data on the dashboards.
- Debugging LocalServer itself (the component log stays with the Greengrass Log Manager and the existing log-analysis feature).
- Remote shell, file browsing or artifact download from the device (the existing Portal features keep that).
- Changing what the executor writes to the device; this feature reads it.
- Alerts on debug content.
- Deployment and AWS resource creation (the owner-gated phase).

## Glossary

Terms from `workflow-telemetry-dashboards` keep their meaning: Portal, Device, Workflow, Pair, Topology, Results_Bucket, Convention_Topic (its root), Publisher_Identity, Attribution, $D, Demo_Scale. From `portal-authorization-and-iam-scoping`: Runbook_Task, Device_Role.

- **Debug_Session**: a Portal record that switches debug on for one Workflow on one or more Devices of a use case (`device_ids`, 1–20) from `enabledAt` until `expiresAt`, with its options, actor and state. States: `pending` (created; at least one Device's shadow write is not yet `ok`), `active` (every shadow write `ok`), `stopped` (ended early by an operator), `expired` (`expiresAt` passed). The ingest tests the Portal state, not the device's.
- **Debug_Shadow**: the named shadow `dda-workflow-debug` of a Device: `desired.sessions.<workflowId>` carries the session the Device must honour; `reported.sessions.<workflowId>` carries what the Device applied.
- **Device_Record**, **usecase_devices**: as the dashboards specification defines them (the Devices-table row naming the use case; the filtered-Scan lookup).
- **Instance**: a UUID the Debug_Agent chooses at each start; with `sessionId` and `seq` it makes every Debug_Message unique across LocalServer restarts.
- **Debug_Agent**: the LocalServer service that applies the Debug_Shadow, builds Debug_Records from the engine's hooks and artifacts, redacts, queues and publishes them.
- **Debug_Record**: one JSON record of type `run`, `log`, `drop` or `session`, carried in a `dda.debug.v1` message.
- **Debug_Message**: one AWS IoT Core message on `dda/debug/{usecase_id}/{workflow_id}/record` holding a batch of Debug_Records, at most 120 KB, identified by (`sessionId`, `instance`, `seq`).
- **Debug_Log_Group**: the CloudWatch Logs group `/dda/workflow-debug/<usecase_id>` in the Portal account.
- **Debug_Stream**: the log stream `<device with ":" replaced by "_">/<workflow_id>` in a Debug_Log_Group (CloudWatch Logs forbids `:` in stream names while IoT allows it in thing names; the name is computed once by `logs_writer.stream_name` and stored on the session's per-Device record); one log event per Debug_Record, each carrying a stable `id` = `<sessionId>:<instance>:<seq>:<index>`.
- **Debug_Viewer**: the Portal page that tails, filters and downloads a Debug_Stream.
- **Redaction**: masking of credentials, tokens, passwords, presigned URLs, authorization headers and connection strings before a record leaves the device, and again before it is written to CloudWatch Logs.
- **Rate_Cap, Byte_Cap, Size_Cap**: at most `maxMessagesPerSecond` Debug_Messages and `maxBytesPerSecond` bytes per second per Device, each message at most 120 KB.
- **Truncation_Marker**: the `truncated` list on a Debug_Record naming each field that was cut and its original size.

## Requirements

### Requirement 1: Purpose and separation

**User Story:** As the owner, I want full-fidelity data from a misbehaving workflow in a log I can read, without touching the dashboards, so that I can find the cause.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. WHILE a Debug_Session is active for a Pair, THE Debug_Agent SHALL send every run's full record (Requirement 4), not a summary, and the engine's log lines for that Workflow.
2. THE debug data SHALL be written only to the Debug_Log_Group of the use case and, when images are on, to the Results_Bucket's `dda-telemetry/debug/` prefix. No dashboard table, tile or chart SHALL read it, and no dashboards route SHALL expose it.
3. THE feature SHALL deploy and work with or without the `workflow-telemetry-dashboards` feature: its own CDK stack, its own IoT rule, its own topic root `dda/debug`, its own Lambda function and its own routes. The shared device-side and Portal modules (D-F: `hooks.py`, `publisher.py`, `control_shadow.py`, `registry_view.py`, `edge_telemetry_common`) SHALL be defined once, in the dashboards design, and used here unchanged; this specification SHALL add nothing to them beyond its own `session_policies` purposes (and SHALL create `session_policies.py` with the dashboards' `ingest` and `media-retention-read` purposes, byte-identical, when it lands first).

### Requirement 2: Session control

**User Story:** As an operator, I want to switch debug on for a device and workflow for a bounded time, see that it is on and when it ends, and switch it off early, so that debugging never runs unattended.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. `POST /debug/usecases/{usecase_id}/sessions` SHALL accept `{workflow_id, device_id | all_devices: true, duration_minutes (5–1440, default 60), level ("info" | "debug", default "debug"), images (boolean, default false), image_max_kb (50–300, default 150)}` and SHALL create **one** Debug_Session whose `device_ids` are the target Devices, with `enabledAt` = now and `expiresAt` = `enabledAt + duration`, one Debug_Shadow entry per target Device and one Debug_Stream per (Device, Workflow). A `device_id` SHALL have a Device_Record naming the use case, else 400 `device_not_recorded` with the dashboards specification's Device_Record_Remedy as its `remedy` (a UseCaseAdmin sets the device's Target architecture on its device page, which calls `PUT /devices/{id}` with `{usecase_id, target_architecture}`; or re-run quick setup); BECAUSE that route needs `node_designer:manage`, which a `MANAGE_DEVICES` holder such as an Operator does not have, THE remedy SHALL say who can perform it.
2. WHEN `all_devices` is true, THE Portal SHALL target the Devices the Portal records as running the Workflow **and** that have a Device_Record for the use case: the Deployments-table query that `workflow_tuning.registered_devices(usecase_id, workflow_id)` performs (the `usecase-deployments-index`, the active statuses, the `dda.workflow.{id}` component-name rule, `target_devices` and `target_device_names`) **∩** `usecase_devices(usecase)`. THE query SHALL be implemented in this feature's own `workflow_debug/targets.py` as a pure function over an injected table, with `DEPLOYMENTS_TABLE` set in the function's environment; it SHALL NOT import `workflow_tuning`, whose module-level imports (`workflow_core.anomaly_invocation`, `workflow_core.catalog.nodes`, `tuning_settings`, `workflows`) reach into the `workflow_core` layer beyond the stdlib-only `stream_url` module that a Python 3.12 function may import (the cp311-only `rpds`), live partly in the ComputeStack's own code asset, and which returns `[]` without `DEPLOYMENTS_TABLE`; the function SHALL return the de-duplicated device list **sorted**, as `workflow_tuning.registered_devices` returns `sorted(devices)`; a unit test SHALL assert that the two functions return the same device list on shared fixtures (the test environment has both on its path). An empty target set SHALL be refused with 400 `no_target_devices` naming the two reasons (no active deployment of the workflow recorded; no device record). A targeted Device that turns out to have no `registered` registration for the Workflow SHALL report `no_registration` and SHALL send nothing; the Debug_Agent SHALL decide this from the shared registration source the dashboards specification defines (`registry_view.py`, which follows the engine's `WorkflowWatcher.registrations_listeners` hook), re-evaluating on every registration change, so that a session started before the workflow is deployed becomes `active` when its registration appears.
3. THE Portal SHALL enforce the caps **all-or-nothing** in one transaction: at most `debugMaxSessionsPerUsecase` (4) active or pending sessions per use case, where a session counts once however many Devices it covers; at most 2 sessions per Device; at most `debugMaxDevicesPerSession` (default 20, range 1–32: the creation transaction needs 3N + 2 DynamoDB actions and `TransactWriteItems` allows 100) Devices per session. A request that would exceed any cap SHALL be refused whole with 409 `debug_session_limit {limit, active}` and SHALL create nothing; a request for a Pair that already has an active or pending session SHALL be refused with 409 `debug_session_exists` naming it.
4. `DELETE /debug/sessions/{session_id}` SHALL stop a session early: the Portal SHALL write `desired.sessions.<workflowId>: null` to each Device's Debug_Shadow (which deletes the key from `desired`) and mark the session `stopped`. BECAUSE a deleted key never appears in `update/delta`, THE Debug_Agent SHALL learn of the removal from the complete `desired` document in `update/documents` (Requirement 5.2) and SHALL stop within 2 s of that message; the runbook SHALL verify the early stop end to end.
5. WHEN `expiresAt` passes, THE Debug_Agent SHALL stop on its own (it needs no message to do so), and THE Portal's maintenance SHALL mark the session `expired` and clear its shadow entries within 5 minutes.
6. THE Portal SHALL show, on the use case's debug page and on the Device's detail page, each session's Workflow, Devices, state, `enabledAt`, `expiresAt`, remaining time, actor, options, and per Device the shadow write result, the Device's `reported` state (`pending`, `active`, `expired`, `stopped`, `no_registration`, `rejected`, or not yet reported) and its counters.
7. THE LocalServer deployed-workflows view SHALL show a "Debug on until <time>" badge per registration from a `debug` field the device run API adds to `GET /workflows/registrations`; this is additive and optional for the first release.
8. THE Portal SHALL audit `debug_session_started` (actor, use case, Devices, Workflow, duration, options), `debug_session_stopped` (actor) and `debug_session_expired`.

### Requirement 3: Permissions

**User Story:** As a Portal administrator, I want enabling to need the device-mutation permission and reading to need the view permission, within the Portal's use-case RBAC.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. `POST` and `DELETE` on sessions SHALL require `Permission.MANAGE_DEVICES` on the use case; `GET` on sessions and on the log SHALL require `Permission.VIEW_DEVICE_LOGS`; the console deep link SHALL require PortalAdmin (`caller_is_portal_admin`).
2. EVERY route SHALL carry the Cognito authorizer, resolve the use case from the path or the stored session, check `rbac_manager.has_permission` before any read or write, answer 403 `{error, error_code: "INSUFFICIENT_PERMISSIONS"}` with an `unauthorized_access` audit row, 500 `registry_unavailable` on `RegistryUnavailable`, and join the Route_Auth_Inventory where it exists. CORS preflight `OPTIONS` methods are the documented exception.
3. THE Debug_Viewer SHALL show only Debug_Streams of use cases the caller holds `VIEW_DEVICE_LOGS` on; the API SHALL build the log group and stream names from the stored session, never from request values.
4. WHILE `PORTAL_REGISTRY_ENFORCED` is off, THE image route (`GET /debug/sessions/{session_id}/images/{execution_id}`) SHALL answer 409 `registry_enforcement_required` with the remedy, because in that mode every signed-in user holds `VIEW_DEVICE_LOGS` on every use case and an image may show people; THE log, session and console-link routes SHALL stay available in that mode, with the exposure recorded (the existing device-logs feature is readable the same way) and shown as a warning on the debug page. THE design SHALL present the two-switch matrix (enforcement on or off) of what the debug page shows, mirroring the dashboards specification's Requirement 17.5, and the "Start debug" modal SHALL say that images are unavailable until enforcement is on.

### Requirement 4: Content

**User Story:** As a developer, I want everything the engine knows about each run, and the engine's own log lines, so that I do not need the device's shell.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. FOR every run of a Pair that finishes while a session is active, THE Debug_Agent SHALL send one `run` Debug_Record with: `executionId`, `status`, the run hook's timing stamps as the dashboards specification's Requirement 2.2 defines them: `enqueuedAtS` (the enqueue time, captured before the executor's RUNNING transition overwrites `started_at`), `acceptedMs` (when the executor picked the run up), `startedMs` (the RUNNING transition, after preflight, frame-source staging and the Triton readiness gate; null for a run refused before it), `finishedMs` (the executor's `finally`), and the derived `queuedMs` (`acceptedMs − enqueuedAtS × 1000`, one-second accuracy), `setupMs` (`startedMs − acceptedMs`, includes the readiness wait; null with `startedMs`), `readinessWaitMs` (the sum of the readiness gate's `waited_seconds` for this execution) and `durationMs` (`finishedMs − startedMs`, pipeline time; null with `startedMs`); `trigger: {source, type, topic, payload (≤ 4 KB, redacted), timestamp, receivedMs}` from the run hook's `trigger_context`, where `type` is derived from the context's shape (`mqtt` when it carries `topic`, `opcua` when it carries `endpoint`, `continuous` when `source` is the continuous runner's, `manual` when it is empty), `source` is the context's `source` or the `type`, `topic` is the MQTT `topic` or the OPC UA `endpoint` + `node_id`, `payload` is the MQTT `payload` or the OPC UA `value`, `timestamp` is the source-side stamp when the context carries one (continuous `frameAcquiredAtMs`; OPC UA `source_timestamp`; for MQTT the delivery `timestamp` × 1000, the only stamp that shape carries), and `receivedMs` is the device-side receipt stamp when the context carries one (continuous `tickAtMs`; MQTT `timestamp` × 1000; null for OPC UA and manual), as the design's §4.2 tables it against the three Trigger_Context shapes the engine writes; a context of **any other shape** SHALL yield `type: "unknown"`, `source` = the context's `source` when it is a string else null, and `payload` = the whole context serialized (≤ 4 KB, redacted), so that a trigger kind added later is visible rather than mis-filed as `manual`; `nodes: [{nodeId, status, durationMs, detail (≤ 1 KB)}]` from the run hook's `node_status` (≤ 64 nodes); `error`, `traceback` (the run hook's `traceback`: the executor's `traceback.format_exc()` captured where the failure was an exception, ≤ 8 KB, redacted; THERE SHALL be no `rawModelOutputs` field, because no engine code writes a raw-model-output key; `bindings: [{nodeId, kind (mqtt | opcua | modbus | …), result (sent | skipped | failed), detail ≤ 1 KB}]` from the binding details; `model: {readiness: [{model, state, waitedMs, ready}], wait}` from the model-readiness hooks the dashboards specification defines (Requirement 2.5 there: the per-run gate's outcomes for this execution, and the continuous runner's current `ModelWait` document or null); `frameSource: {kind, cameraId, width, height, frameSeq, frameTs}` (from the run hook's `trigger_context` (`source`, `frameSeq`, `frameAcquiredAtMs` for continuous runs) and the `stream` and `frame` entries `_seed_stream_metadata` writes into the run's `tag_values`; null when neither is present); `artifacts: {outputDir, captureId, hasOverlay}`; `logs` (Requirement 4.2); and `image: {bucket, key, width, height, bytes}` when images are on.
2. THE run's log lines SHALL travel **only inside its `run` record**, as `logs: [{ts, level, logger, message (≤ 2 KB, redacted), src}]`: the INFO-and-above lines of the loggers `run.log` captures (`run_log.CAPTURED_LOGGERS`: `workflow_engine` and `gstreamer`) read from the run's `run.log` at assembly (`src: "runlog"`; complete and thread-independent, because `RunLogCapture` captures every line of those two loggers during the run) **through a specified parser** (`runlog.parse`): BECAUSE `run_artifacts.read_run_log` returns one text blob (the rolled `.1` backup, then the live file, up to 512 KB each) in the format `run_log._LOG_FORMAT` (`%(asctime)s %(levelname)s %(name)s: %(message)s`) whose `asctime` is **local time** (`logging.Formatter`'s default converter) and whose tracebacks and multi-line messages are unprefixed continuation lines, THE parser SHALL match each line against that format, SHALL convert `asctime` to epoch milliseconds **as local time** (so the merge with the tap's epoch stamps is not misordered by the host's UTC offset), SHALL append a non-matching line to the previous entry's `message` (or open a first entry with level INFO and logger `run.log` at the run's `accepted_ms` when none exists yet, for a cut first line of the rolled file), SHALL cap the entries at 2,000 or 256 KB per run oldest first with the overflow counted in `truncated` as `logs.runlog`, and SHALL pass every entry through the same redaction as a tap line (the lines were already redacted on the device by `RunLogCapture`'s redaction filter; the second pass is idempotent); a unit test SHALL parse a fixture with a two-line message and a traceback, and a second SHALL format a synthetic `LogRecord` with `run_log._LOG_FORMAT` and parse it back to the same fields, so a format change breaks the test; plus every other line the live tap attributed to the run (`src: "tap"`): the DEBUG-level lines of those two loggers during a DEBUG session, and **every line of the `dda_triton` logger at any level**, because `run.log` never captures that logger although its `model_readiness` module writes the readiness story (INFO and WARNING) on the executor thread during the run's readiness gate. The skip rule "already in `run.log`" SHALL apply only to INFO-and-above records of loggers in `run_log.CAPTURED_LOGGERS`. During a DEBUG session THE Debug_Agent SHALL lower to DEBUG those of the three loggers whose effective level is above DEBUG, SHALL remember which ones it lowered and the level each had, SHALL on every agent tick re-assert DEBUG on a lowered logger whose level something else has changed (`RunLogCapture.stop` restores the level it saved at the run's start), adopting the level it found as that logger's new saved level, and at the session's end SHALL restore a logger's saved level only while its current level is still DEBUG, so that a run spanning the session's start leaves the logger at its pre-run level and never permanently at INFO (a unit test with the root at WARNING); and SHALL install a filter that drops the DEBUG records **of exactly those lowered loggers** on **every other handler attached to them or to their ancestors**, including the `RunLogCapture` handler of every run (through an additive `run_log.CAPTURE_FILTERS` hook that `RunLogCapture.start` applies to its handler, empty by default), so that neither `run.log` nor the Greengrass component log changes by a single byte in either direction (a logger an operator had already set to DEBUG keeps its DEBUG lines in both); a unit test SHALL run an identical run with and without a DEBUG session and compare the `run.log` bytes, and a second SHALL pre-set `gstreamer` to DEBUG and assert its DEBUG line still reaches the root handler during a DEBUG session. THE tap's handler SHALL do on the emitting thread only what cannot be done elsewhere: read the two context variables (`CURRENT_EXECUTION`, `CONTINUOUS_RUN`, which are per-thread context and must be read where the record is emitted), call `record.getMessage()` (the plain `%` formatting every handler performs; deferred formatting would read `args` that the caller may mutate afterwards, which is why `logging.handlers.QueueHandler.prepare` formats on the emitting thread too) capped at 4 KB, capture `(created, levelno, name, message, thread ident, exc_info)` into the agent's bounded inbox (drop-oldest, counted) and return; attribution by content (open execution ids, registration ids), traceback formatting, redaction and the 2 KB cap SHALL run on the agent's thread (Requirement 7.3).
3. THE Debug_Agent SHALL send a `drop` Debug_Record for each trigger firing the gate discards and each continuous tick skipped for the Pair (`{ts, nodeId, reason}`), coalesced to at most one per reason per second with a `count`.
4. THE Debug_Agent SHALL send a `session` Debug_Record when a session starts, every 30 s while it is active, and when it ends (`{state, reason, counters: {records, bytes, queueDrops, tapDrops, truncations, rateDeferred, imagesSent, imagesCapped, imagesTooLarge, publishFailures, unattributedLines, otherPairLines}, agentVersion}`).
5. WHERE `images` is on, THE Debug_Agent SHALL upload one JPEG per run (the overlay when present, else the base capture), downscaled to within 960 × 540 and re-encoded at quality 75 and then 55 until under `image_max_kb`, to `dda-telemetry/debug/<usecase_id>/<device>/<workflow_id>/<sessionId>/<executionId>.jpg` in the Results_Bucket, in the bucket's `region` carried by the session entry, at most 2 per second and 2,000 per session (then `imagesCapped`), and SHALL put the key in the `run` record; a frame still above `image_max_kb` after the quality-55 re-encode SHALL NOT be uploaded, SHALL be counted `imagesTooLarge`, and SHALL leave `image: null`; the upload SHALL use the Device_Role's `AllowInferenceResultsUpload` statement, never the hand-attached policy.
6. THE Debug_Agent SHALL build the `run` record from the run hook's `RunRecord` (which carries the execution row's fields, `capture_id`, `output_dir`, `log_path`, the trigger context, the node statuses, the tag values, the detections and the traceback) and from the artifacts the executor already writes (`{captureId}.json`, `{captureId}.jsonl` and `.detections.json` through `detections.read_detections(output_dir, capture_id)`, `run.log`), and SHALL NOT change what the executor writes: the artifacts' bytes are identical with and without a session (Requirement 4.2's filter guarantees it for `run.log`).

### Requirement 5: Delivery of the switch without a deployment

**User Story:** As an operator, I want to switch debug on from the Portal and have the device pick it up within seconds, or on reconnect, without any Greengrass deployment.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. THE Portal SHALL deliver a session by writing `desired.sessions.<workflowId> = {sessionId, usecaseId, enabledAt, expiresAt, level, images, imageMaxKb, bucket, region, prefix, maxMessagesPerSecond, maxBytesPerSecond}` to each target Device's `dda-workflow-debug` shadow through `iot-data:UpdateThingShadow` (via the shared `credentials.client('iot-data', usecase, 'debug-control')`), and SHALL stop it by writing `null` for that key. `region` SHALL be the **bucket's** region as the dashboards specification resolves it (Requirement 10.2 there: BECAUSE nothing in the repository creates the default Results_Bucket, which an operator creates in any region, the `x-amz-bucket-region` header of the media-location readiness probe's `ListObjectsV2` response for **every** Results_Bucket, default or override, stored as `media_location.region` with `region_source`, and the use case's region only as the fallback when the header is absent), so that the agent's image `PutObject` never meets `PermanentRedirect`; when the dashboards' Telemetry_Settings do not exist for the use case, THE debug API SHALL perform the same probe itself through its `media-retention-read` credential path and apply the same header-first rule, SHALL report `region_source` in its 201 answer, and SHALL use the use case's region only when the header is absent. `usecaseId` (a UUID, required) is the use case the Portal attributes the session to; THE Debug_Agent SHALL build its publish topic `dda/debug/{usecaseId}/{workflowId}/record` from it and SHALL validate `prefix` against it, so that the feature needs no other source of the use case id (it must work without the dashboards' `dda-workflow-telemetry` shadow, Requirement 1.3); the ingest's attribution checks the topic's use case against the Device_Record as the second gate.
2. THE Debug_Agent SHALL use the shared `control_shadow.py` protocol: subscribe over IPC to `$aws/things/{thing}/shadow/name/dda-workflow-debug/#`, publish an empty `.../get` when the subscription becomes active, apply `desired.sessions` **only from complete documents** (`get/accepted` and `update/documents`, in shadow-version order; `update/delta` only triggers a fresh `get`), treat a `<workflowId>` key absent from the complete `sessions` map as a stopped session, and write `reported.sessions.<workflowId>` (`sessionId`, `state`, `appliedAt`, `instance`, counters) through `.../update` when a session's state changes and otherwise at most once per 5 minutes.
3. WHEN a session entry arrives, THE Debug_Agent SHALL activate it within 2 s if `expiresAt` is in the future and ignore it (reporting `expired`) otherwise; the expiry SHALL count from `enabledAt` as the Portal wrote it, so a Device that was offline when the session started gets only the remaining time. WHEN the entry disappears from the complete document, THE Debug_Agent SHALL end the session within 2 s with a final `session` record (`reason: stopped`).
4. THE Debug_Agent SHALL NOT depend on the ShadowManager `synchronize` list, a recipe change, a component configuration change or any deployment. The one LocalServer release that carries the agent is the only deployment this feature needs.
5. WHILE no session is active for a Pair, THE Debug_Agent SHALL hold no handler on the loggers for that Pair and SHALL publish nothing for it.
6. THE device-side session states SHALL be `pending` (entry applied, registration not yet found), `active`, `no_registration` (no `registered` registration for the Workflow after 30 s; re-evaluated on every registration change through the shared registration source, so it flips to `active` when one appears and back when the registration leaves `registered`), `rejected` (invalid entry, with a reason, including a `maxBytesPerSecond` outside 32 KB/s–1 MB/s), `expired` and `stopped`; each change SHALL be reported. AFTER a LocalServer restart, THE Debug_Agent SHALL resume every session still in the future from the `get` with a new `instance`, `seq` restarting at 1, and a `session` record with `reason: resumed`.

### Requirement 6: Sink and viewer

**User Story:** As the owner, I want the debug data viewable as a log in the Portal, near-live, with filters and a download, and a console link for admins.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. THE design SHALL evaluate CloudWatch Logs through the IoT rule CloudWatch Logs action, CloudWatch Logs through the Greengrass Log Manager component, CloudWatch Logs through an IoT rule Lambda action, S3 plus Athena, and a device-local log viewed in the LocalServer UI, with citations, and SHALL choose CloudWatch Logs written by the Lambda action (D-B) with its reasons.
2. THE sink SHALL be the Debug_Log_Group `/dda/workflow-debug/<usecase_id>` in the Portal account (created when the first session of a use case starts, retention `debugLogRetentionDays`, default 14, range 1–90, tagged `dda-portal:usecase-id`) and the Debug_Stream named by `logs_writer.stream_name(device, workflow_id)` (`<device>/<workflow_id>` with any `:` in the thing name replaced by `_`, since CloudWatch Logs forbids it; computed once at session creation, stored on the session's per-Device record and read back by the API, never rebuilt from request values; the ingest uses the same function), one log event per Debug_Record whose **event timestamp is the ingest's write time** (`writeTs`, one value per batch, stamped just before `PutLogEvents`), so that the stream is ordered by arrival at the sink and an event written late (an out-of-order asynchronous invocation, a retried invocation, a concurrent container) always lands at the end, where a tail finds it; the record's own `ts` (device time) and the IoT receive time `receivedMs` SHALL travel inside the JSON for display and filtering. THE ingest SHALL stamp every event with a stable `id` = `<sessionId>:<instance>:<seq>:<index>` (the record's position in its message) and with `writeTs` and `receivedMs` before writing it.
3. `GET /debug/usecases/{usecase_id}/log?device_id=&workflow_id=&from=&to=&next=&execution_id=&node_id=&level=&type=` SHALL read the Debug_Stream with `GetLogEvents` over the **write-time** window `[from, to]` (default the last 15 minutes; `to − from ≤ 24 h`), or with `FilterLogEvents(logGroupName, logStreamNames=[<the Pair's stream>], filterPattern, startTime, endTime, nextToken)` over the same window when `execution_id` is given, SHALL apply the other filters server-side on the JSON records (`node_id`, `level`, `type`; `ts` bounds when `ts_from`/`ts_to` are given), SHALL return at most `limit` events per call (`limit` 1–10,000, default 2,000; the viewer's drain uses the default and the download passes 10,000, the API maximum, so that 50,000 events are at most 5 calls), each with its `id`, `writeTs`, `receivedMs` and `record`, plus a continuation token `next` for continuing **within the same window** (the download's paging), and SHALL cache identical calls for 2 s. **Quota pacing:** BECAUSE `GetLogEvents` (10 or 25 TPS by Region) and `FilterLogEvents` (5, 10 or 25 TPS by Region) are **account-wide, non-adjustable** quotas shared with the Portal's Build Fleet, device-logs, plugin-build, git-sync and training log readers, THE API SHALL take a token from a per-container bucket before every CloudWatch call (`GetLogEvents` at most `debugGetLogEventsPerSecond` = 4 per second, `FilterLogEvents` at most `debugFilterLogEventsPerSecond` = 2 per second, both deployment values) and, when the bucket is empty, SHALL answer **429 `log_read_throttled {retry_after_ms}` without calling CloudWatch**; a `ThrottlingException` that still arrives SHALL be mapped to the same 429 with `retry_after_ms: 3000`; the design SHALL table the two quotas with their Region split and the cross-feature blast radius, and RB-1 SHALL record the Portal Region's two values. **Termination rule for `next`:** BECAUSE `GetLogEvents` never returns a null token (at the end of the stream it returns the same token that was passed in), THE API SHALL answer `next: null` when the response's `nextForwardToken` equals the request's `next` **or** the response carries zero events, and the response's `nextForwardToken` otherwise; for `FilterLogEvents`, `next` SHALL be the response's `nextToken` when present and `null` when the response omits it (which is how that API signals the end). A stub test SHALL feed a `GetLogEvents` response whose `nextForwardToken` equals the request token and assert `next: null`, and a `FilterLogEvents` response without `nextToken` and assert the same. WHEN no session (any state, within its 30-day record TTL) exists for the Pair, THE API SHALL answer 404 `no_debug_session`. A rejected `next` (the stream was recreated) SHALL answer 400 `invalid_token`.
4. THE Debug_Viewer (`/debug/{usecase_id}/{workflow_id}/{device_id}`) SHALL tail by polling every 3 s while visible with **an overlapping write-time window**: each poll asks for `from = max(lastSeenWriteTs − 60 s, tailStart)` to `to = now`, follows `next` within that window until it is exhausted (at most 3 calls of `limit=2000` per poll), de-duplicates by `id` against a bounded set of the last 10,000 ids it has shown, and **on a 429 `log_read_throttled` SHALL back off to a 10 s poll** (returning to 3 s after two clean polls) with a visible notice; it SHALL never rely on the forward token across polls, because the token is a position in the stream's time order and an event written late would sit behind it. The 60 s overlap covers write-time skew between concurrent ingest containers (sub-second) with a wide margin; a late write of any age lands at the end of the stream by Requirement 6.2 and is found by the next poll, so **no event is missed by the tail** and the invariant "every event shown exactly once" holds through `id` de-duplication. The viewer SHALL order the shown events by the record's `ts` (then `id`), inserting a late-arriving event at its place in time and marking it "late" when its `writeTs − receivedMs` exceeds 60 s. It SHALL filter by execution id, node, level, record type and time, SHALL render `run` records expanded (nodes table, detections table, bindings, error, the `logs` list) and `log` records as lines, SHALL show the image when a record carries one (a presigned GET valid 120 s, minted by the API after the permission check and the enforcement guard of Requirement 3.4), and SHALL download the shown range as NDJSON assembled client-side (at most 50,000 events, paged with `next` within the window at `limit=10000` and **at most 2 calls per second**, pausing for `retry_after_ms` on a 429). No event SHALL be shown twice and no event SHALL be lost at a window boundary.
5. `GET /debug/sessions/{session_id}/console-link` SHALL return, for a PortalAdmin, the CloudWatch console URL of the Debug_Stream in the Portal account's region, with the group and stream names encoded as the logsV2 console expects (`urllib.parse.quote(name, safe="")` with `%` replaced by `$25`, so `/` becomes `$252F`; one example pinned by a unit test), and 403 otherwise.
6. THE viewer SHALL never connect to a device-local server.

### Requirement 7: Safety

**User Story:** As the owner, I want debug mode to be unable to slow runs, flood IoT, leak secrets or run up a bill.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. THE Debug_Agent SHALL publish at most `maxMessagesPerSecond` (default 10, range 1–20) Debug_Messages **and at most `maxBytesPerSecond` bytes** (default 262144 = 256 KB/s, range 32 KB/s–1 MB/s; the deployment value `debugMaxBytesPerSecond` with the same default, written into the session entry) per second per Device, enforced by two token buckets in the batcher, batching records into one message up to 100 KB; records that cannot be sent at either cap SHALL wait in the queue (`rateDeferred`, `bytesDeferred` counted) and SHALL be dropped oldest-first when the queue is full. BECAUSE every component's publishes share the Greengrass nucleus's one in-memory MQTT spooler (`mqtt.spooler.maxSizeInBytes`, 2.5 MB by default, which rejects new messages when full), THE byte cap SHALL default low enough that a session cannot fill it on a healthy uplink, and WHEN the nucleus rejects a message with its spooler full, THE Debug_Agent SHALL drop that batch (counted `publishFailures` and `spoolFull`, never retried) and SHALL back off its sending (doubling its interval up to 10 s, halving back on success), so that a debug session never silences the dashboards' Summaries from the same device; the runbook SHALL record the device's measured uplink and the nucleus's configured spooler size before a session's caps are chosen.
2. EVERY Debug_Message SHALL be at most 120 KB (within IoT's 128 KB); a `run` record larger than the budget SHALL be truncated in this order until it fits: `log` lines attached to it (tap lines first, then runlog lines, oldest first; the runlog side is already capped at 2,000 entries or 256 KB by the parser, Requirement 4.2), `outputs.detections` beyond 100, `outputs.llm.*.text` and `outputs.bedrock.*.text` beyond 1 KB each, `trigger.payload` beyond 1 KB, `traceback` to its last 2 KB, `nodes[].detail` to 200 characters; each cut SHALL add `{field, originalBytes}` to `truncated`.
3. THE Debug_Agent SHALL use a bounded asynchronous queue of 500 records that drops the oldest and counts `queueDrops`; the run hook SHALL enqueue a reference and return within 2 ms at p95 (unit benchmark); **the log tap's handler SHALL capture and enqueue within 50 µs per record at p95** for a 200-character message (the same benchmark, extended; the tap's inbox is a separate bounded drop-oldest deque of 5,000 entries whose drops are counted `tapDrops`); record assembly, content attribution, redaction and serialization SHALL run on the agent's thread (Requirement 4.2 names the only work the emitting thread does); the runbook SHALL measure the p50 run latency on the MIC-730 with and without an active DEBUG session with images off, and the difference SHALL be under 3 %.
4. THE Debug_Agent SHALL redact, before publishing, in every string of every record: URL user information, the values of secret query parameters (`password`, `pass`, `pwd`, `token`, `secret`, `key`, `signature`, `X-Amz-Signature`, `X-Amz-Credential`, `X-Amz-Security-Token`), `Authorization`, `Proxy-Authorization`, `X-Api-Key` and `Cookie` header values, connection strings (`scheme://user:pass@host`, `password=`, `pwd=`, `AccountKey=`, `SharedAccessKey=`), AWS access key ids (`AKIA…`, `ASIA…`) and secrets (`aws_secret_access_key`), and every Credential_Store value, with the vendored `stream_url.redact(text, secrets)` rules as the base; THE ingest function SHALL apply the same rules again before `PutLogEvents`. THE redaction module SHALL be **environment-neutral** so that the device copy and the Portal copy can be byte-identical: it SHALL import `redact` as `try: from workflow_core.stream_url import redact except ImportError: from workflow_engine.vendor.workflow_core.stream_url import redact` and SHALL take `secrets` as an argument (the device passes `dda_logging.redaction.credential_store_secrets()`, the Portal passes an empty set), with the byte-identity test over the two files; a unit test SHALL feed each pattern through both and assert the masked form. BECAUSE the Portal name `workflow_core.stream_url` exists only in the `workflow_core` Lambda layer, THE stack SHALL build that layer (`backend/layers/workflow_core`, the ComputeStack's exclude list, runtimes 3.11 and 3.12) and attach it to the ingest function (Requirement 11.6); a jest test SHALL assert the attachment, and an import-boundary unit test SHALL import `workflow_debug.redact` in a subprocess whose `sys.path` lacks `backend/layers/workflow_core/python` and assert `ImportError`, so that the dependency is pinned rather than masked by `conftest.py`.
5. THE Debug_Agent SHALL publish through the shared IPC client with `PublishToIoTCore` under `mqttproxy:2`, QoS 1, and SHALL make no AWS SDK call except the image `PutObject`.
6. THE Portal SHALL cap sessions per use case and per Device (Requirement 2.3) and SHALL refuse `duration_minutes` above 1440.
7. THE ingest function SHALL drop, and count as `session_inactive`, Debug_Messages whose `sessionId` is not a Portal session in state `pending` or `active` with `expiresAt > now` covering the Pair (cached 30 s), so that a device that does not stop cannot write past its session, and SHALL drop messages over the Rate_Cap by more than 2× as `rate_exceeded`.
8. THE design SHALL estimate the cost of one debug-hour at the PPE demo's rate, at the caps, and with images on, with cited unit prices.

### Requirement 8: Ingestion and attribution

**User Story:** As a Portal operator, I want debug data attributed to exactly one Device and use case before it is written, so that no device can write into another use case's log.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. THE IoT rule `dda_dbg_ingest` (Portal-account copy `dda_dbg_ingest_portal`; use-case-account copy in `usecase-account-stack.ts`, condition-gated) SHALL match exactly `dda/debug/+/+/+`, SQL `SELECT encode(*, 'base64') AS p, topic() AS t, principal() AS pr, clientid() AS c, timestamp() AS r, newuuid() AS m`, with one Lambda action invoking the Portal account's `dda-portal-workflow-debug-ingest` function through the alias `acct-<account>` and a CloudWatch Logs error action in the rule's account.
2. THE ingest SHALL attribute as the dashboards' ingest does, through the shared `edge_telemetry_common` package: the sending account from the alias in `invoked_function_arn`; the topic's use case must exist in that account and region; the certificate thumbprint must resolve (through `credentials.client('iot', usecase, 'ingest')`) to a thing that equals the client id (less a `#<n>` suffix); the Device's Device_Record must name the topic's use case; the Workflow must belong to it; the payload's `deviceId` and `workflowId` must match; and a Debug_Session must cover the Pair (Requirement 7.7). Rejections SHALL be counted by reason and never logged with a payload.
3. THE ingest SHALL validate the `dda.debug.v1` envelope (`schema`, `sessionId`, `instance` (UUID), `deviceId`, `workflowId`, `workflowVersion`, `seq`, `records[]` of 1–200 records, each with `type`, `ts`), SHALL redact again, SHALL stamp each record's `id` (Requirement 6.2), SHALL `PutLogEvents` the records in chronological order to the Debug_Stream (creating the stream on first use), SHALL retry a throttled `PutLogEvents` three times with backoff and then raise so Lambda retries the invocation, and SHALL de-duplicate by (`sessionId`, `instance`, `seq`) for 10 minutes **with two distinct DynamoDB calls**: a strongly consistent `GetItem` of the `SEQ#<instance>#<seq>` item **before** `PutLogEvents` (present → `duplicate`, counted and dropped; absent → continue, nothing written) and a conditional `PutItem` of that item (`attribute_not_exists`, 10-minute TTL) **only after** a successful `PutLogEvents`, so that a Lambda retry after a failed `PutLogEvents` finds no item, writes the batch and is never counted `duplicate` (a failed condition on the put is a DEBUG line: a concurrent container wrote the same batch, which the viewer's `id` de-duplication absorbs); a stub test SHALL make `PutLogEvents` throttle four times and assert that the function raises and writes no `SEQ#` item, that a second invocation of the same event writes the batch and is not `duplicate`, and that a third invocation after the success is `duplicate`. A LocalServer restart (a new `instance`, `seq` back at 1) never collides with the previous instance's messages.
4. THE ingest SHALL emit metrics (`Received`, `Accepted`, `Rejected` by reason, `BytesWritten`) in embedded metric format and SHALL alarm on the on-failure queue depth. BECAUSE Lambda stores asynchronous-invocation configuration per qualifier and the rules invoke the `acct-<account>` aliases, THE retry settings (`retryAttempts: 2`, `maxEventAge` 1 h) and the on-failure SQS destination SHALL be configured **on every alias** (`alias.configureAsyncInvoke(...)`), never on the unqualified function alone; a jest test SHALL assert one `AWS::Lambda::EventInvokeConfig` per alias whose `Qualifier` is that alias's name and none on the unqualified function; THE ingest role SHALL hold `sqs:SendMessage` on the on-failure queue (Requirement 11.1).

### Requirement 9: Cost

**User Story:** As the owner, I want to know what a debug-hour costs and that the caps bound it.

#### Acceptance Criteria

1. THE design SHALL estimate a debug-hour at: the PPE demo's rate (about 1.7 runs per second, about 8 KB per `run` record including the log lines it carries); the default byte cap (256 KB per second, the most a session can sustain unless the deployment value is raised); the message cap (10 messages per second of 120 KB, labelled as a ceiling that assumes an uplink of about 10 Mbps); and the demo's rate with images on; naming the uplink each scenario implies; including the shadow operations of the session (the Portal's writes and the agent's `reported` writes at their stated cadence) and the ingest's DynamoDB footprint of **1 RRU + 2 WRU per message** (the consistent `GetItem` dedup check, the conditional `SEQ#` put after `PutLogEvents` and the counters update, Requirement 8.3); with unit prices cited to the Price List files or pricing pages read on a stated date.
2. THE design SHALL state the monthly cost of the feature when no session is active (near zero: no polling, no rule evaluations, the log groups' storage only).
